# Acquisition Manager — Offer Call Script

**Purpose:** Present offer, negotiate, and get the contract signed. This is where deals close.

---

## Intro

> "Hey [Seller], this is [Name]. [LM name] scheduled me to give you a call at this time — is it still good for you?"

---

## Set the Stage

> "Thanks for taking my call. I know you have a lot of other important things going on. To get into the call, I just want to touch on what it looks like if you do decide to work with us."
>
> "If you decide that you would like to move forward with us at the end of this call, then we would send over our written agreement to your email and we would both go through that together."
>
> "Are you currently in the position to be able to do that?"

**If NOT able to:**
> "Okay, I understand. When would be a better time to talk? That way, we can do everything at once."

**If YES:**
> "At the end of our conversation, all I ask for is a confident yes or no, and if it's a no, that's perfectly fine too. Seem fair? *(Let them answer)*"
>
> "And with that being said, anything other than a yes we will just assume it's a no."

---

## Address Roadblocks

**If there are other decision makers:**
> "I know you said that [Name] has input on the decision. Are they present with us right now?"

**If YES / Decision makers present:**
> "Let's just say in a perfect world we agree on a price and the agreement looks good — what would happen next? What would the next steps be for you?"

**If personal (sole decision maker):**
> "It sounds like you are the only decision maker in the process of selling this house."

---

## State & Dig Into Motivation

> "I know when we spoke a bit earlier you [were feeling/struggling with — INSERT MOTIVATION]. I just need a little bit more clarity on this so I can better understand your situation."

**Probing questions:**
- *"How long has that been going on?"*
- *"How has that impacted you?"*
- *(Mirror the last 3 words of their sentence)*
- *"It seems like..."*
- *"It feels like..."*
- *"It sounds like..."*
- *"If we were able to solve [their problem], how would that make you feel?"*
- *"Paint me your picture-perfect scenario."*
- *"What were you hoping I might be able to do for you?"*

**Personal example (foreclosure):**
> "Based on your conversations with Daniel, it sounds like this situation has been difficult for you. Do you know how many months you're behind on the mortgage?"
>
> "Sounds like the faster we're able to close the better, correct?"
>
> "Did you have a place lined up you were planning to buy or rent?"
>
> "Would it be beneficial to have 45 days to live in the house with no payments while you find a place to move to?"
>
> "I know when we bought Tim's house in Kissimmee we went through a similar situation. We were able to get his mortgage paid off along with some post-occupancy time to find a new place."

---

## Setting Up the Offer

> "Thank you for all of that. So now getting into what we would like to be at for the property."
>
> "Keep in mind this is what you will walk away with **IN YOUR POCKET** minus your mortgage and prorated taxes (which is a state thing). You do not have to pay closing costs, you do not pay any realtor commissions, the property will be sold **AS-IS** which means you do no repairs and no cleaning. As well as of course solving [their issue/problem]."

**Personal (foreclosure):**
> "As well as of course getting the house out of foreclosure and giving you a fresh start."

---

## Giving the Price

*(Start 30k less than No Access MAO)*

> "With that being said, I have approval from underwriting and construction to buy this property at **$[SPECIFIC NUMBER]**."

**If they need to think about it:**
> "Hey [Mr. Seller], I just want to recap our conversation. This offer achieves how you want the property sold — which is in its current condition without having to make repairs, and without you having to go weeks without knowing if an offer is coming in."
>
> "This offer also closes on the timeline that makes the most sense for you — which is ASAP. While also giving you 45 days to live in the property for free while you find a place that makes the most sense for you."
>
> "[Mr. Seller], I understand you were wanting more money for it, but are you willing to sacrifice one of those other items to get that price?"

---

## Negotiating

### If "Price is too low"

> *\*Sigh\** "Hmmmmm, just off the record, what were you hoping for at least?"

> "If you didn't sell it to us, what would be your plan?"

> "Are you able to push this off for 30 days? If that's the case, it would take another 30 days or so to get it closed. What do you think the bank would say?"

### If "I want to shop around"

> "Can the motivation wait that long?"

### Price Rebuttals (at least 1-2 before moving to MLS MAO)

> "We might not be the best fit. What do you think we should do?"

> "So then even if I was able to come up to [price below No Access MAO], that still probably wouldn't be enough?"

> "Okay, I understand [range] doesn't make sense. So let's just say in a perfect world, if we were able to do [final range closer to MAO], that still wouldn't work?"

---

## MLS/Wholesale Pitch (35k Profit)

*Frame as a partnership where their part is holding the house longer and allowing reasonable access. You bring expertise on construction, capital, and selling so you can get it sold for higher than the guaranteed cash offer.*

### Final Price Increase (Moving to MLS pitch)

> "It seems like $[X] is an important number for you to get. So I do believe I have a way I can possibly get that for you — I just need you to be flexible with me on one thing: **additional access to the property**."

**"What do you mean by additional access?"**
> "All I mean by that is you allow us to get access to the property at pre-scheduled times so we can allow our financial partners and some retail investors, accompanied by agents, to do some quick walk-throughs."
>
> **OR**
>
> "We would need just about 4 one-hour time slots per week within our due diligence period to allow some of our financial partners and retail investors, who are accompanied by agents, to walk through the property."

**"Who are these retail investors?"**
> "The retail investors are people we partner with on projects like this when the numbers are stretched for us because they have different exit strategies that allow them to pay more money for properties."

**"Why do you need this much access?"**
> "This allows us to present the property to more of our retail investors and financial partners who are accompanied by agents, that way we can exercise another option of ours and give you the price you're wanting."

**"What if the retail investors don't like the property?"**
> "Well, the agreement would be just between you and our company and has nothing to do with the retail investors we work with. Our finance department would never allow me to make you an offer that they weren't confident we could execute on. The only times we've had problems with this have been instances where the seller describes the property to be in one condition, and it's in a completely different condition when we go out there."

**If they say NO to access:**
> "Okay, we can still work together without you allowing us the additional access, but our offer will be at [No Access MAO] and we can close on your timeline."

---

## If Offer Accepted

> "Alright, perfect — Congrats!"
>
> "Now I just need your email to send the agreement to, so we can go over it together."

---

## If No Offer Accepted

> "Well [Name], I do appreciate you taking the time to speak with me. Sorry we couldn't get you what you were looking for. Please reach out to me if anything changes."

---

## Post-Offer Objections

**"Call me in [X] days"**
> "Okay, no problem... but when I call you back, what questions should I have the answers for?"

**Seller can't give a yes or no answer:**
> "Okay, like I mentioned before, all we need is a confident yes or no. Anything other than a yes we usually take as a no. It seems like right now it's a no, which is perfectly okay."

**Seller says "No":**
> "Thank you so much for being honest with me. Off the record, what were you hoping to get?"
>
> "I'm not saying I can get $X approved — what would happen next if I could?"
>
> "Do you think there's any way I could get a couple of pics? If I can show those to my boss, then I have a better chance at getting closer to your number."

**Not all decision makers are present:**
> "Okay, I'm sorry — I guess I'm confused. When I asked if there were any other decision makers, you said it was just you/and [Name]."

**"We were wanting [higher price]":**
> "I understand, and that makes sense. We might not always be the best fit. That's probably your best price?"

**"We want to get a couple of other offers":**
> "Okay, it seems like price is very important to you."
>
> *(Scorch the earth — look out for EMD and DD. Remind them we buy 8-10 properties a month and what we can offer today might not be the same tomorrow. We don't have unlimited funds.)*

**"We had an offer for a little more":**
> "Okay, that's awesome. When are you set to close?"
>
> **If they tell you the competing offer:**
> "Okay, so if we can come up to that, what would happen next?"

**"The best I can do is $X":**
> *(If you know it's a deal):*
> "Okay, can I place you on a brief hold while I talk to my manager? He's about to leave the office — let me just talk to him real quick."

---

## Scorching the Earth

*If we haven't gotten to our Novation MAO yet:*
> "[Mr. Seller], there are two things I want you to look out for that can tell you how serious someone is: 1) their EMD and 2) their DD. If they have a very low EMD and high DD, that's a good indication they don't actually have the funds to buy your property and are just going to shop it around. Also, what did they say when you asked them how long they've been in business?"

*If we are close to our Novation MAO:*
> "[Mr. Seller], I just want to make sure nobody is wasting your time because I know there are a lot of people out there that give investors like us a bad name. So if you would, ask them for some testimonial reviews and verify they have a website. This way you can hear what other people say about them. You can also ask for their closing attorney and call them up to confirm that they actually do purchase properties."

---

## Common Objections

**"I can go through the agreement myself":**
> "Hey [Mr. Seller], it's our company policy to go through the agreement with you, just so there are no questions and misunderstandings that would delay the process. Like you told me earlier in the call, you're in a position currently to do this."

**"I don't even know when I could get time for access":**
> "Hmmmm okay, well what were you hoping for in terms of access? In the past... *(go into third-party story and set the expectations we want)*"

**"Are you just going to sell to someone else?"**
> "Of course we're going to sell to someone else. Now, [Mr. Seller], what I can promise you is we're gonna buy your house on a timeline you want at the price we agreed upon. Our company will be doing that. Now, what we do after that? I don't have any decision-making ability in that. I just know we're going to get you the price you want at the time you want it. But to answer your question, of course we're going to sell this eventually to someone else — we don't keep these as rentals. But that will not affect our transaction between you and our company at all."

**"I can make more money listing on the market":**
> "I agree with you. If you have the time and energy to do so, that's your best option to get the most money possible. We're not always the best fit."

**"I have a tenant and don't want to disturb them":**
> "Yes, we can buy the property with the tenant in place..."

**"This is happening quickly / I don't make rash decisions":**
> *(With tonality):* "I understand completely, [Mr. Seller]. Now, when I asked you about if you were able to go over the agreement today, you said you could... Is there something I messed up on?"
>
> **If NO:** "I understand completely. It's a big decision for you. What should I do next?"
>
> **If they ask for follow-up:** Double down on setting expectations for the next call.

**"I don't want to sign anything where the language isn't clear":**
> "I understand. We will go through the agreement together. If there are any things you do not like, I will more than likely be able to get them removed, pending my manager's approval."

**"That's quite a bit lower than I was looking for":**
> "Hmmmmmmm... Okayyyyy... off the record, what were you hoping for at least?"

**"Go look at the property and then give me a better offer":**
> "Usually we first get an agreement in place, and then during our due diligence go out there and inspect the property."

**"The agreement is 5-10 pages long":**
> "Yes, all agreements have to be okay'd by a lawyer to make them legally binding. Is that okay with you? That's the purpose of this call — to go through everything together and address anything we need to."

**"Give me some time to study the agreement":**
> "But when I asked if you were in a position to go over it, you said yes. But now you're saying you need some time to go over it — was there something I said that was wrong?"

**"I need to discuss this with my wife/husband/brother/etc.":**
> "Oh okay, I thought you told me earlier there was no one else that would influence this decision. Did I do something wrong or say something to mess this up?"

**"I want a decent offer":**
> "When you say a decent offer, what do you mean by that?"

**"Why can't you just tell me your number?":**
> *(Third-party story):* "In the past, when we've told people our offer, they've just taken it and shopped it around to get a higher price. We aren't saying you would do that — that's just what's happened in the past."

**Seller is just shopping around:**
> "If you're not ready to move forward and just want us to give you more information, we might not be the best fit because that will turn into us bothering you for more information. And if you're anything like me, you're just going to be overwhelmed with all the info I send you. So maybe it's best for us not to do anything until you're ready to do something."

**"Why would you list on the MLS?":**
> "We do this because not every property fits our buying criteria, but again, the service we provide to our clients is to get them the money they want and make sure they're able to get it. This just allows us to give that ability to provide this service no matter what underwriting says about the property. If we go this route, there will be a little more paperwork I'll have to come to you and get signed. We can go in the property as little as 2 times, but preferably more so we can have a higher probability of getting you the $[XXX,XXX] in your pocket."

**"I need to talk to my attorney!":**
> "I understand that you need to talk to your attorney. This is a big decision."
>
> "But usually when someone tells me they have to talk to their attorney, it's because I didn't explain the process very well. Is that the case?"
>
> "Or it's because they don't feel comfortable working with us. What would you need to see to feel more comfortable working with us?"
>
> "Oh okay, it's neither of those two — then it must be something with the price or terms of the deal?"

**"What happens if you sell it for more than X?":**
> "You know, [Mr. Seller], I'm not exactly sure as that's up to our finance department, but what I can tell you is that if you don't get paid your [purchase price], we don't get paid either."
>
> "So if you don't get paid, neither do we. We take our fee from the back end of the deal, meaning we take a fee from the buyer that we find for you. And also keep in mind that you're still paying no realtor commissions, closing costs, attorney fees, etc. We will take care of all of that."

---

## Manager Pitch (15k Profit)

*Only for fringe deals (less than 10k projected profit)*

> "Okay so [Mr. Seller], it seems like $[X] is a very important number for you to get, and there could be a way for us to still work together and get you your price. Would you be interested in hearing how that would work?"

> "Okay great. So for starters, [Mr. Seller], to give it to you in simplest terms, essentially what we would be doing is providing a service to you at **ZERO COST**. More specifically, we would be making it our priority to help you find a buyer willing and able to pay your full asking price."

> "Let me make myself clear that **NOTHING would change** in terms of this being an all-cash deal at closing, and you still would not be coming out of pocket for **ANYTHING** in terms of closing costs, realtor commissions, attorney fees, etc."

> "So before I continue, is everything I'm saying so far making sense?"

> "Okay great. So to kinda get into some more detail, what we would be doing consists of two main things:"
>
> "**First**, we would market the property out on your behalf to our exclusive, private list of investors across the [City] market. We make it our job to know every cash buyer across the [Metro] area. These investors range from your weekend warriors all the way up the food chain to institutional investors representing hedge funds. These investors can typically pay more than us as they're generally more buy-and-hold oriented."
>
> "And **simultaneously**, we would also list the property on the MLS to get more exposure to the transaction and get some more eyeballs on it — again, to increase the likelihood of us being successful in finding you a buyer willing to pay your full asking price."

> "So again — before I continue — do you have any questions I can answer? Or is there anything about this process that you're not comfortable with?"

> "In order for this to work, all we ask for in exchange is that you allow the interested parties we receive inquiries from to have the ability to access the property."

> "With that being said, any and all forms of access are going to be scheduled through you (and the tenants, etc.) and confirmed on the day of. So let me make myself clear when I say the last thing we would do is have you host an open house with 50-60 people walking through it. These will be investors and retail buyers accompanied by agents who have been pre-vetted and pre-qualified through our closing team and are serious about making offers."

> "With that in mind, we just need a **MAXIMUM of 21 days TOPS** in order to get an answer."

> "So — to address the worst-case scenario — we come to you within that 21-day time period and say, 'Hey [Mr. Seller], we marketed this thing hard every day, but unfortunately we were unable to find someone willing to pay your full price. However, here's what our highest and best offers look like.' And it would be completely up to you on whether or not you'd like to proceed with the sale. So if that's a no, we cut you loose, shake hands, and walk away as friends."

> "On the other hand, the **best-case scenario** being that we come to you within that time frame and say, 'Hey [Mr. Seller], great news — we have a buyer willing and able to pay your full price, and we're good to go for closing as scheduled.'"
