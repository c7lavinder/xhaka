## 1.1 NAH Mission & Values

### Who We Are
**New Again Houses Nashville** is a real estate investment company that buys properties directly from homeowners. We provide a fast, hassle-free selling option for people who need to sell quickly or have properties that aren't ideal for the traditional market.

### What We Do
- Buy houses for cash
- Close quickly (often 2-4 weeks)
- Purchase properties "as-is" (no repairs needed)
- Handle all paperwork and closing costs

### Our Values
- **Speed** — We move fast because sellers need fast solutions
- **Integrity** — We do what we say we're going to do
- **Transparency** — No hidden fees, no surprises
- **Respect** — Every seller deserves to be treated well

### Why Sellers Choose Us
| Traditional Sale | Selling to NAH |
|-----------------|----------------|
| 3-6 months | 2-4 weeks |
| Repairs required | As-is purchase |
| Showings & open houses | One walkthrough |
| 6% agent fees | No fees |
| Deal can fall through | Cash, guaranteed close |

## 1.3 Deal Flow Overview

### The Big Picture (From Process Map)

```
Campaign → Lead → Research → Contact → Appointment → Walkthrough → Offer → Contract → TC → $$$
                                              ↓
                                        [Follow Up]
```

### The 8 Phases

| Phase | Name | Owner | Key Action |
|-------|------|-------|------------|
| 1 | Lead Generation | Data Manager / Cold Callers | Build campaigns, source leads |
| 2 | Research & Qualification | Automation + LM | Verify data, qualify lead |
| 3 | Initial Contact | LM | Call leads, assess interest |
| 4 | Appointment Setting | LM | Book and confirm walkthroughs |
| 5 | Walkthrough | AM | Visit property, evaluate |
| 6 | Offer | AM | Present offer, negotiate |
| 7 | Contract | AM | Get signatures, close deal |
| 8 | Follow Up | LM + Automations | Nurture non-converts |

### Phase Details

#### Phase 1: Lead Generation (Data Manager + Cold Callers)
- Build marketing campaigns
- Import/clean lists in BatchLeads
- Cold call and text campaigns
- Submit qualified leads to pipeline

#### Phase 2: Research & Qualification (Automations + LM)
- Verify lead data is accurate
- Research property details
- Automation updates Podio/GHL
- Lead marked ready to call

#### Phase 3: Initial Contact (LM)
- Call new leads (20/day target)
- Qualify interest and motivation
- Working leads vs. ghosted
- Move interested leads forward

#### Phase 4: Appointment Setting (LM)
- Book walkthrough appointments
- System sends pre-apt confirmation
- Confirm appointments
- Stage 2 Complete → handoff to AM

#### Phase 5: Walkthrough (AM)
- Attend scheduled walkthrough
- Evaluate property condition
- Take notes/photos
- Schedule offer call
- Stage 3 Complete

#### Phase 6: Offer (AM)
- Call seller at scheduled time
- Present cash offer
- Handle objections, negotiate
- Accept → Contract OR Follow Up

#### Phase 7: Contract (AM)
- Send contract for signature
- Walk seller through terms
- Get all signatures
- Send to Title Company
- Handoff to Dispo

#### Phase 8: Follow Up (LM + Automations)
- Hot/Warm/Cold follow-up tiers
- Drip campaigns for non-converts
- Re-engage when ready
- Survey call to close out dead leads

### Key Decision Points

1. **Lead?** — Valid lead to pursue?
2. **Researched?** — Enough info to call?
3. **Answered?** — Did they pick up?
4. **Interested?** — Motivated to sell?
5. **Show Up?** — Attended walkthrough?
6. **Accepted?** — Ready for offer call?
7. **Accept or Follow Up?** — Did they accept the offer?
8. **Contact?** — Should we keep trying?

*See `sops/nah-acquisitions-process-map.md` for full visual documentation.*

### Where It Happens
| Stage | Where in GHL |
|-------|--------------|
| New Lead | Contacts → Smart Lists |
| Qualified | Opportunities → Acquisitions Pipeline |
| Apt Set | Calendar + Opportunity status |
| Under Contract | Opportunity → UC stage |
| Dispo Active | Opportunity → Dispo pipeline |
| Closed | Opportunity → Won |

## 2.4 Cold Caller Process

### Daily Workflow
```
Start Shift
    ↓
Load list in dialer
    ↓
Call through the list
    ↓
├── Answer → Qualify → Submit Lead (if interested)
├── No Answer → Leave voicemail, next
├── Bad Number → Skip/remove
└── DNC Request → Mark and remove
```

### Calls Per Day Target
**20+ calls per day per rep** (quality conversations, not just dials)

### Cold Call Script
```
"Hi, is this [NAME]?

This is [YOUR NAME] calling from New Again Houses. 
We're a local company that buys houses in the Nashville area.

I'm reaching out because we're interested in buying properties 
in your neighborhood. Would you have any interest in selling [ADDRESS]?"
```

### Qualifying on the Call
Listen for:
- **Motivation:** Why would they sell?
- **Timeline:** How soon?
- **Condition:** What shape is the property in?
- **Decision maker:** Can they make the call?

### When to Submit Lead
Submit to LM pipeline if:
- ✅ Expressed interest in selling
- ✅ Has a timeline (even if vague)
- ✅ Property is in our buy area
- ✅ They are owner/decision maker

### When NOT to Submit
- ❌ Zero interest
- ❌ Property outside our area
- ❌ Already listed with agent (unless flexible)
- ❌ Unrealistic price expectations (unless motivated)
- ❌ DNC request

## 3.3 When New Leads Come In

### What Happens at 🟢 New Leads Created
When a lead enters the system:

```
🟢 New Leads Created
    ↓
[GHL] Lead lands in "New Lead" column
    ↓
[Data Manager] Cleans data, verifies info
    ↓
[Data Manager] Moves lead to "Warm Lead" or "Hot Lead" column
    ↓
[AUTOMATION] Stage change triggers:
    ├── Assigns lead to LM
    └── Sends notification to LM
    ↓
[YOU - LM] Now call the lead
            ↓
          Qualify New Lead Task
```

### Your Job: Call When Assigned
- **You wait** for Data Manager to clean and move the lead
- Once moved to Warm/Hot column, you get assigned + notified
- GHL notification = your cue to engage
- **Weekend note:** Speed-to-lead slows down when Data Manager isn't working

### How Data Manager Classifies Warm vs Hot
**5-Factor Scoring:**
1. Timeline — Ready to sell soon?
2. Condition — Property needs work?

## 4.3 Property Analysis

### What You're Calculating
1. **ARV (After Repair Value)** — What's it worth fixed up?
2. **Repair Costs** — How much to get it there?
3. **MAO (Maximum Allowable Offer)** — Highest you can pay

### The Formula
```
MAO = ARV × 0.70 - Repairs - Assignment Fee
```

**Example:**
- ARV: $200,000
- Repairs: $30,000  
- Target assignment fee: $15,000
- MAO = $200,000 × 0.70 - $30,000 - $15,000 = **$95,000**

### Running Comps
1. Find 3-5 comparable sales (same area, similar size)
2. Adjust for differences (bed/bath, sqft, condition)
3. Average to get ARV
4. Document your analysis

### Repair Estimation
| Category | Low | Medium | High |
|----------|-----|--------|------|
| Cosmetic (paint, carpet) | $5-10k | $10-20k | $20-30k |
| Kitchen | $5-10k | $15-25k | $30-50k |
| Bathrooms (each) | $3-5k | $5-10k | $10-20k |
| Roof | $5-8k | $8-12k | $12-20k |
| HVAC | $3-5k | $5-8k | $8-15k |
| Foundation | $5-10k | $10-25k | $25-50k+ |

## 5.8 Assignment vs Double Close

### Assignment
- Assign contract directly to buyer
- Buyer pays assignment fee at closing
- Simplest, fastest method
- Buyer sees your profit (assignment fee)

### Double Close
- You close with seller, then immediately with buyer
- Two separate transactions
- Buyer doesn't see your profit
- More complex, higher costs
- Used when spread is large or buyer sensitive

### When to Use Which
| Situation | Method |
|-----------|--------|
| Normal deal, trusted buyer | Assignment |
| Large spread ($20k+) | Consider double close |
| Buyer asks about your profit | Double close |
| Speed is critical | Assignment |

## 6.2 Lead Data Entry & Cleanup

### The Handoff Flow
```
New Lead Created
    ↓
[GHL] Lands in "New Lead" column
    ↓
[YOU] Clean/verify data:
    - Name correct?
    - Phone valid?
    - Property address accurate?
    - Any notes from source?
    ↓
[YOU] Score using 5 factors:
    - Timeline
    - Condition
    - Price
    - Motivation
    - Source
    ↓
3+ factors? → "Hot Lead" column
< 3 factors? → "Warm Lead" column
    ↓
[AUTOMATION] Assigns LM + sends notification
```

### Data Fields to Verify
| Field | Check For |
|-------|-----------|
| Contact Name | Spelling, completeness |
| Phone | Valid format, callable |
| Property Address | Full address, correct format |
| Source | Where lead came from |
| Initial Notes | Any context from caller/form |

### Adding to MasterSuite
When lead is cleaned:
1. Add property to MasterSuite with available details
2. Link MasterSuite record in GHL notes (if applicable)
3. This enables property evaluation later

## 6.8 Common Issues & Fixes

### Duplicate Leads
**Problem:** Same lead entered twice
**Fix:** Merge in GHL (keep the one with more history)

### Missing Data
**Problem:** Lead came in with incomplete info
**Fix:** Note what's missing, move to Warm (not Hot), LM can gather on call

### Wrong Assignment
**Problem:** Lead assigned to wrong LM
**Fix:** Reassign in GHL, notify correct LM

### KPI Discrepancy
**Problem:** Numbers don't match between GHL and spreadsheet
**Fix:** Pull GHL report, reconcile, update spreadsheet

## 8.8 Offer Calculator

### Purpose
Worksheet for calculating offer prices.

### Formula
```
MAO = ARV × 0.70 - Repairs - Assignment Fee

Where:
- MAO = Maximum Allowable Offer
- ARV = After Repair Value
- 0.70 = 70% rule (adjust for market)
- Repairs = Estimated rehab cost
- Assignment Fee = Your profit ($10-20K typical)
```

### Example
```
ARV: $200,000
× 0.70 = $140,000
- Repairs: $30,000
- Assignment Fee: $15,000
= MAO: $95,000
```

📄 **Full template:** `templates/offer-calculator.md`

# LM Quick-Start Guide
**Role:** Lead Manager
**Your Job:** Turn leads into appointments
**Day 1 Read Time:** 10 minutes

---

## Your Mission
Get sellers on the phone → Qualify their motivation → Book walkthrough appointments for Kyle (AM)

You're the filter. Most leads won't convert. Your job is to find the ones that will.

---

## The Numbers
- **10-20%** of contacts → Appointment (ready now)
- **80-90%** of contacts → Follow-up pool (not ready yet)
- **Goal:** 20+ calls/day, 3+ appointments/week

---

## Your Daily Flow

### Morning
1. Check GHL notifications — any new leads assigned?
2. Review today's follow-up tasks
3. Start calling

### Calling Priority
1. **New assigned leads** (moved to Warm/Hot by Jessica)
2. **Hot follow-ups** (1-month bucket)
3. **Warm follow-ups** (4-month bucket)
4. **Scheduled callbacks**

### End of Day
1. Log your numbers in KPI spreadsheet
2. Update lead statuses in GHL
3. Set tomorrow's follow-up tasks

---

## The Call (30-Second Version)

```
1. "Hey [Name], this is [You] with New Again Houses..."
2. "I probably caught you at a bad time?"
3. Verify property → "Still own [Address]?"
4. Check motivation → "What's got you thinking about selling?"
5. If motivated + ready → Set walkthrough appointment
6. If motivated + not ready → Schedule follow-up
7. If not motivated → Polite exit, tag appropriately
```

**The key question:** *"What's got you thinking about selling?"*
- More pain = better lead
- "Just curious" / "hearing offers" = weak motivation

---

## Setting Appointments

### Good Appointment = 5 Things
1. ✅ True motivation (real pain)
2. ✅ Discounted price expectation (used anchor)
3. ✅ Walkthrough scheduled (in-person)
4. ✅ Offer call scheduled (follow-up call)
5. ✅ Decision makers available

### When You Book
1. Confirm date/time
2. Confirm who'll be there (all decision makers)
3. Enter in GHL calendar
4. Automations handle confirmations

---

## Tools You'll Use

| Tool | What For |
|------|----------|
| GHL | Calls, texts, pipeline, calendar |
| Google Chat | Team alerts, apt notifications |
| KPI Spreadsheet | Daily number entry |

---

## Quick Reference Numbers

**Price Anchor:** 50-60% of Zillow Zestimate
- "Other investors buying similar properties for around $X..."

**Follow-Up Buckets:**
- 1-Month = Motivated, close to ready
- 4-Month = Interested, needs time
- 12-Month = Not motivated, minimal effort

**Disqualify Fast If:**
- "You called me first" + no pain
- Wants full retail price, won't budge
- Hostile and no motivation detected

### End of Day

## The Walkthrough (Quick Version)

```
1. Arrive 5 min early
2. Greet warmly → "Thanks for having me out"
3. Tour the property room by room
4. Note: roof, HVAC, foundation, major repairs
5. Take photos (with permission)
6. Ask: "Anything I should know about the property?"
7. Confirm: "When's a good time for me to call with numbers?"
8. Schedule offer call before you leave
```

**Never make an offer at the walkthrough.** Always schedule a separate call.

---

## The Offer Call (Quick Version)

```
1. Recap walkthrough → "I took a look at everything..."
2. Present the math → ARV, repairs, your offer
3. Pause. Let them respond.
4. Handle objections
5. Negotiate if room exists
6. Close → "If we can agree on $X, I can send the contract today"
```

**The pause is powerful.** State your number and wait.

---

## The Math

```
MAO = ARV × 0.70 - Repairs - Assignment Fee

Example:
ARV: $200,000
× 0.70 = $140,000
- Repairs: $30,000
- Assignment: $15,000
= MAO: $95,000
```

Know your numbers cold. Sellers respect confidence.

# Objection Handling Library
**Purpose:** Ready responses for common seller pushback
**Roles:** LM, AM
**Status:** Draft — Feb 3, 2026

---

## How to Use This

1. **Learn the patterns** — Most objections fit categories
2. **Practice out loud** — Reading isn't enough
3. **Keep it handy** — Reference during calls until it's automatic
4. **Adapt to your style** — These are frameworks, not scripts

---

## The Golden Rule

**Objections aren't rejection — they're requests for more information.**

When someone objects, they're still on the phone. That's interest.

---

## Price Objections

### "That's too low"

**What they mean:** Your anchor/offer is below their expectation

**Response:**
> "I totally understand — and that's not our offer, just what I'm seeing other cash investors pay for similar properties. What were you hoping to get?"

**Then:** Let them give a number. Now you have their anchor.

---

### "I won't go below $X"

**What they mean:** They have a floor in mind

**Response:**
> "Got it. Help me understand — is that based on what you owe, what you need to move forward, or what you think it's worth?"

**Then:** Understand the WHY behind their number. Often it's not the house value — it's what they need.

---

### "I need to get my price"

**What they mean:** They're anchored to a specific number

**Response:**
> "What would getting that price allow you to do? I want to make sure we're solving the right problem here."

**Then:** Focus on their goal, not the price. Sometimes creative terms beat higher offers.

---

### "You called me first" (refusing to give price)

**What they mean:** They want you to bid against yourself

**Response:**
> "Totally fair. If we were able to buy it completely as-is, cover all closing costs, and close on your timeline — do you have even a rough range in mind? Even $10-15K range helps me know if we're in the same ballpark."

**Then:** The range question is less threatening than asking for exact price.

---

### "Zillow says it's worth $X"

**What they mean:** They're using online estimates as truth

**Response:**
> "Zillow's a starting point, but it doesn't know about [condition/repairs/neighborhood specifics]. When we run actual comps on recently sold properties nearby, the numbers look different. Would you like me to walk you through what we're seeing?"

**Then:** Educate with real data. Don't trash Zillow — just explain its limits.

---

## Timeline Objections

### "I'm not ready to sell yet"

**What they mean:** Timing isn't right now

**Response:**
> "Totally understand. When do you think you might be ready? I'd rather stay in touch and help when the time's right than push you into something early."

**Then:** Schedule follow-up, add to appropriate bucket (1mo/4mo/12mo).

---

### "I need 60-90 days to move"

**What they mean:** They need time, but aren't opposed to selling

**Response:**
> "That's no problem at all. We can write a 60-day closing, or even longer if needed. If we got an agreement in place now, you'd have certainty while you figure out your move. Would that help?"

**Then:** Offer flexibility. Time is cheap for us, valuable for them.

---

### "I need to wait for probate"

**What they mean:** Legal process hasn't completed

**Response:**
> "We work with probate situations all the time. We can actually get an agreement in place now and just close once probate clears. That way you're not starting from scratch later. Would that be helpful?"

**Then:** Show you understand the process. Reduces their anxiety.

---

### "I want to wait until [season/spring/market improves]"

**What they mean:** They think waiting will get them more

**Response:**
> "I hear that a lot. Here's what I've seen — markets are unpredictable, and carrying costs add up. What if we could get you a number now that works, and you don't have to wait and wonder? At minimum, you'd know your cash option."

**Then:** Plant seeds about certainty vs. hoping for better.

---

### "My tenant needs 60 days notice"

**What they mean:** Tenant complicates timing

**Response:**
> "That's totally fine. We can write a 60-day closing into the contract. Or if you'd prefer, we can buy it with the tenant in place — we work with occupied properties all the time."

**Then:** Show flexibility. Tenant situations aren't dealbreakers.

---

## Decision Maker Objections

### "I need to talk to my [spouse/partner/kids]"

**What they mean:** They can't decide alone (or want an out)

**Response:**
> "Absolutely — this is a big decision. When do you think you could connect with them? I'd love to schedule a time when you're both available so we can answer any questions together."

**Then:** Get the decision maker on a call. Don't let it go into a black hole.

---

### "My brother/partner is on the deed"

**What they mean:** Multiple owners involved

**Response:**
> "Got it. When would be a good time to get everyone together? We can do a quick call — even a three-way call works — so everyone hears the same information."

**Then:** All decision makers present = deals close. Missing one = deals die.

---

### "I need to think about it"

**What they mean:** Could be real, could be an exit

**Response:**
> "Totally fair — it's a big decision. What specifically would you want to think through? Maybe I can help with info now so you have everything you need."

**Then:** Uncover the real objection. "Think about it" is often hiding something else.

---

## Trust Objections

### "How do I know this isn't a scam?"

**What they mean:** They're cautious (smart)

**Response:**
> "Great question — you should be careful. We're [Company Name], local investors here in [City]. You can look us up. We never ask for money upfront, and you'd work with a real title company for closing. Happy to send you info to verify."

**Then:** Be transparent. Offer proof. Don't get defensive.

---

### "I'm already working with a realtor"

**What they mean:** They have another path

**Response:**
> "No problem at all. How's that going for you? I ask because a lot of folks come to us after trying the traditional route — our offer is usually faster and more certain, even if the number's different."

**Then:** Don't compete — compare. If they're happy with their agent, wish them well.

---

### "I've gotten other offers"

**What they mean:** They're shopping (good sign)

**Response:**
> "That's great — you should explore your options. What have those offers looked like? I want to make sure we're competitive, and if we're not the best fit, I'll tell you."

**Then:** Get intel on competition. Sometimes you'll beat them, sometimes you won't.

---

### "Why should I sell to you?"

**What they mean:** Convince me

**Response:**
> "Fair question. Three things: We close fast — usually 2-3 weeks. We buy as-is — no repairs or cleaning. And we're local — you're not dealing with some faceless company. If those things matter to you, we're a good fit. If not, I'll point you elsewhere."

**Then:** Be direct about your value prop. Confidence sells.

---

## Motivation Objections

### "I'm just hearing offers"

**What they mean:** Low motivation, shopping

**Response:**
> "Got it. What would make an offer interesting enough to actually consider? I don't want to waste your time if we're not in the right ballpark."

**Then:** Qualify harder. This is often a time-waster. Either find real motivation or move on.

---

### "I'm not really looking to sell"

**What they mean:** No urgency

**Response:**
> "No worries — I appreciate you taking my call. Just out of curiosity, is there anything happening with the property or in your life that might change that down the road?"

**Then:** Plant seeds. If nothing's there, polite exit and long-term follow-up.

---

### "I love my house, I'd never sell"

**What they mean:** Emotional attachment

**Response:**
> "Totally get it — it's your home. I'll leave you alone. But if anything ever changes — life happens — keep my number. No pressure, ever."

**Then:** Graceful exit. Don't push emotional sellers. They're not ready.

---

## Closing Objections

### "I need to sleep on it"

**What they mean:** Delay tactic or genuine processing

**Response:**
> "Of course. When you wake up tomorrow, what would make you say yes? Let's make sure I've answered everything before you think it over."

**Then:** Identify what's missing. Address it now if you can.

---

### "Send me something in writing"

**What they mean:** They want documentation (or an out)

**Response:**
> "Absolutely. What specifically would you want to see? I'll put together exactly what you need."

**Then:** Clarify expectations. Then follow through quickly.

---

### "That's my bottom line"

**What they mean:** Final offer (maybe)

**Response:**
> "I appreciate you being direct. Let me take this back and see what we can do. If we can make it work at your number, are you ready to move forward?"

**Then:** Test commitment. If yes at their number → you know your ceiling.

---

## The "RED" Seller

**RED = Hostile, rude, irritable**

### Signs
- Short, aggressive responses
- "That's none of your business"
- Angry tone, interrupting

### Strategy
1. **Don't match their energy** — Stay calm and professional
2. **Acknowledge** — "I hear you, and I don't want to waste your time"
3. **Find the pain** — Often they're frustrated about something else
4. **Give them an out** — "If this isn't a good time, I'm happy to call back"

### When to Walk Away
If you sense ZERO motivation underneath the hostility → polite exit.

> "I appreciate your time. Doesn't sound like we're a fit right now, but if that ever changes, we're here."

---

## Third-Party Story Technique

**Use when:** They have a problem and need to see you've solved it before

**Framework:**
> "We had another seller who was in the exact same situation — [describe their problem]. What we did was [your solution], and they were able to [positive outcome]. Would something like that work for you?"

**Examples:**
- Probate delays → "We've helped families through probate, started paperwork early, closed the day it cleared"
- Tenant issues → "We bought a property last month with a difficult tenant, took care of everything"
- Needed moving time → "Seller needed 90 days, we wrote it into the contract, no rush"

---

## Daily Practice

1. **Pick 3 objections** from this list
2. **Say your responses out loud** — alone or with a partner
3. **Role play** — Have someone throw objections at you randomly
4. **Review your calls** — Note which objections tripped you up
5. **Refine** — Adjust language to fit your voice

Objection handling is a skill. It gets better with reps.

---

*Update this library as you discover new objections and responses that work.*

# FILE: faq.md


# Frequently Asked Questions
**Purpose:** Quick answers to common questions
**Status:** Draft — Feb 3, 2026

---

## General

### What does NAH do?
We buy houses directly from sellers for cash, then sell them to investors (flippers, landlords). We're wholesalers — we find deals and connect them with buyers.

### How do we make money?
Assignment fees. We get a property under contract at price X, sell our contract to a buyer at price Y. The difference (Y - X) is our profit. Typical: $10-20K per deal.

### What's the difference between assignment and double close?
- **Assignment:** We sell the contract itself. One closing. Buyer sees our fee.
- **Double Close:** Two closings — we buy, then immediately sell. Buyer doesn't see our fee. Used when spread is large.

---

## Leads & Pipeline

### How do leads come in?
Multiple sources:
- Cold calls (our callers reach out)
- SMS campaigns
- Webforms (sellers contact us)
- Pay-per-lead services
- Referrals

### What makes a "hot" lead vs "warm"?
5-factor scoring: Timeline, Condition, Price, Motivation, Source.
- **3+ factors = Hot**
- **< 3 factors = Warm**

### How fast should we contact new leads?
As fast as possible once Data Manager moves them to Warm/Hot. Speed to lead matters — first to connect often wins.

### What's the follow-up system?
Three buckets:
- **1-Month:** Motivated, close to ready. Call frequently.
- **4-Month:** Interested, needs time. Call occasionally.
- **12-Month:** Not motivated. Minimal effort, just stay in touch.

---

## Calls & Scripts

### Do I have to follow the script exactly?
Use it as a framework, not a straitjacket. The structure matters — the exact words can be your own style. But don't skip sections, especially motivation.

### What if the seller is hostile?
Stay calm. Don't match their energy. Try to find the underlying pain. If there's zero motivation + hostility, politely exit. Some people aren't worth the fight.

### Should I make offers on calls?
**LMs:** Never. Your job is to set appointments.
**AMs:** Only on offer calls, after walkthrough. Never during walkthrough.

### What's the price anchor?
50-60% of Zillow Zestimate. Example: "Other investors are buying similar properties around $X..." This sets expectations before they give their number.

---

## Appointments & Walkthroughs

### What makes a "good" appointment?
Five things:
1. True motivation (real pain)
2. Discounted price expectation
3. Walkthrough scheduled (in-person)
4. Offer call scheduled (follow-up)
5. All decision makers available

### What if the seller no-shows?
Try calling immediately. If no answer, follow up 2x that day, then next day. If still nothing, move to follow-up bucket.

### What do I do at a walkthrough?
Build rapport → Tour property → Take notes/photos → Assess repairs → Confirm motivation → Schedule offer call. **Don't make an offer at the walkthrough.**

### How long should a walkthrough take?
30-60 minutes typically. Don't rush, but don't linger. Get what you need and set the next step.

---

## Offers & Contracts

### How do I calculate my offer?
```
MAO = ARV × 0.70 - Repairs - Assignment Fee

Example:
ARV: $200,000
× 0.70 = $140,000
- Repairs: $30,000
- Assignment: $15,000
= MAO: $95,000
```

### What if my offer gets rejected?
Ask what would work for them. Try to understand the gap. If negotiation fails, put in follow-up — circumstances change.

### When is a deal "under contract"?
When the seller signs the purchase agreement. At that point it moves to Dispo for buyer blast.

### How long do we have to close?
Typically 30-60 day contracts. Some flexibility for seller needs. The key is having enough time to find a buyer without rushing.

---

## Buyers & Disposition

### How do we find buyers?
Built over time:
- Networking
- Buyer inquiries on deals
- Real estate meetups
- Online (Facebook groups, etc.)

### What's a Tier A buyer?
Verified funding, fast response, has purchased from us before. These buyers get deals first.

### How do I blast a deal?
Prepare summary (address, beds/baths, ARV, repairs, asking price, photos) → Send to Priority buyers → Then Qualified → Track responses.

### What if no buyers are interested?
Reprice if possible. Re-blast with adjusted terms. Consider JV with a partner who has different buyers. Last resort: potentially walk from the deal.

---

## Tools

### I'm locked out of GHL, what do I do?
Ask Corey to reset. Don't create new accounts.

### What's the difference between GHL and MasterSuite?
- **GHL:** CRM — contacts, pipeline, conversations, calendars, automations
- **MasterSuite:** Property evaluation — comps, ARV, repair estimates, deal math

### Where do I enter my daily numbers?
KPI Spreadsheet → your role's Spotlight tab. LMs use LM Spotlight, AMs use AM Spotlight, etc.

### How does Gunner work?
Calls recorded in GHL → Gunner pulls recordings → AI grades on criteria → Scores appear in Gunner dashboard. Check weekly to improve.

---

## Team & Communication

### Who do I ask if I have questions?
- **LM questions:** Kyle or Corey
- **AM questions:** Corey
- **Dispo questions:** Esteban or Corey
- **Data/GHL questions:** Jessica or Corey
- **Anything urgent:** Corey

### How should I use Google Chat?
- @mention when you need someone specific
- Don't spam — be concise
- Apt alerts auto-post — just acknowledge if needed
- Check it frequently during work hours

### What's the response time expectation?
- Direct @mention: Within 30 minutes
- General updates: Read when you can
- Urgent issues: Immediately

---

## Common Mistakes

### Talking too much on calls
Let the seller talk. Your job is to listen for motivation. Ask questions, then shut up.

### Making offers at walkthroughs
Never. Always schedule a separate offer call. It gives you time to run numbers and creates a decision point.

### Not updating GHL
If it's not in the system, it didn't happen. Update lead statuses, log notes, keep pipeline current.

### Chasing bad leads too long
Disqualify fast. Time on bad leads = time not spent on good ones. If there's no motivation after 2-3 attempts, move on.

### Skipping the script structure
The script exists because it works. Especially for motivation — don't rush past it.

---

## Troubleshooting

### Lead stuck in wrong stage
Check if automation should have moved it. If not, manually update. Note why in the record.

### Numbers don't match between GHL and spreadsheet
Pull a GHL report, reconcile manually, update spreadsheet. Note discrepancy.

### Seller wants to back out after contract
Talk to Corey immediately. Options depend on situation — sometimes negotiable, sometimes you let it go.

### Buyer trying to renegotiate after UC
Hold firm if possible. If they walk, re-blast to other buyers. Don't give away profit unnecessarily.

---

## Still Have Questions?

1. Check the relevant module first
2. Search the glossary
3. Ask your supervisor
4. If truly stuck, ask Corey

Most questions have answers somewhere in the training. Use it.
