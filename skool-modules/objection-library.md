## The Golden Rule

**Objections aren't rejection — they're requests for more information.**

When someone objects, they're still on the phone. That's interest.

---

## Price Objections

### "That's too low"

**What they mean:** Your anchor/offer is below their expectation

**Response:**
> "I totally understand — and that's not our offer, just what I'm seeing other cash investors pay for similar properties. What were you hoping to get?"

**Then:** Let them give a number. Now you have their anchor.

---

### "I won't go below $X"

**What they mean:** They have a floor in mind

**Response:**
> "Got it. Help me understand — is that based on what you owe, what you need to move forward, or what you think it's worth?"

**Then:** Understand the WHY behind their number. Often it's not the house value — it's what they need.

---

### "I need to get my price"

**What they mean:** They're anchored to a specific number

**Response:**
> "What would getting that price allow you to do? I want to make sure we're solving the right problem here."

**Then:** Focus on their goal, not the price. Sometimes creative terms beat higher offers.

---

### "You called me first" (refusing to give price)

**What they mean:** They want you to bid against yourself

**Response:**
> "Totally fair. If we were able to buy it completely as-is, cover all closing costs, and close on your timeline — do you have even a rough range in mind? Even $10-15K range helps me know if we're in the same ballpark."

**Then:** The range question is less threatening than asking for exact price.

---

### "Zillow says it's worth $X"

**What they mean:** They're using online estimates as truth

**Response:**
> "Zillow's a starting point, but it doesn't know about [condition/repairs/neighborhood specifics]. When we run actual comps on recently sold properties nearby, the numbers look different. Would you like me to walk you through what we're seeing?"

**Then:** Educate with real data. Don't trash Zillow — just explain its limits.

## Timeline Objections

### "I'm not ready to sell yet"

**What they mean:** Timing isn't right now

**Response:**
> "Totally understand. When do you think you might be ready? I'd rather stay in touch and help when the time's right than push you into something early."

**Then:** Schedule follow-up, add to appropriate bucket (1mo/4mo/12mo).

---

### "I need 60-90 days to move"

**What they mean:** They need time, but aren't opposed to selling

**Response:**
> "That's no problem at all. We can write a 60-day closing, or even longer if needed. If we got an agreement in place now, you'd have certainty while you figure out your move. Would that help?"

**Then:** Offer flexibility. Time is cheap for us, valuable for them.

---

### "I need to wait for probate"

**What they mean:** Legal process hasn't completed

**Response:**
> "We work with probate situations all the time. We can actually get an agreement in place now and just close once probate clears. That way you're not starting from scratch later. Would that be helpful?"

**Then:** Show you understand the process. Reduces their anxiety.

---

### "I want to wait until [season/spring/market improves]"

**What they mean:** They think waiting will get them more

**Response:**
> "I hear that a lot. Here's what I've seen — markets are unpredictable, and carrying costs add up. What if we could get you a number now that works, and you don't have to wait and wonder? At minimum, you'd know your cash option."

**Then:** Plant seeds about certainty vs. hoping for better.

---

### "My tenant needs 60 days notice"

**What they mean:** Tenant complicates timing

**Response:**
> "That's totally fine. We can write a 60-day closing into the contract. Or if you'd prefer, we can buy it with the tenant in place — we work with occupied properties all the time."

**Then:** Show flexibility. Tenant situations aren't dealbreakers.

---

## Decision Maker Objections

### "I need to talk to my [spouse/partner/kids]"

**What they mean:** They can't decide alone (or want an out)

**Response:**
> "Absolutely — this is a big decision. When do you think you could connect with them? I'd love to schedule a time when you're both available so we can answer any questions together."

**Then:** Get the decision maker on a call. Don't let it go into a black hole.

---

### "My brother/partner is on the deed"

**What they mean:** Multiple owners involved

**Response:**
> "Got it. When would be a good time to get everyone together? We can do a quick call — even a three-way call works — so everyone hears the same information."

**Then:** All decision makers present = deals close. Missing one = deals die.

---

### "I need to think about it"

**What they mean:** Could be real, could be an exit

**Response:**
> "Totally fair — it's a big decision. What specifically would you want to think through? Maybe I can help with info now so you have everything you need."

**Then:** Uncover the real objection. "Think about it" is often hiding something else.

---

## Trust Objections

### "How do I know this isn't a scam?"

**What they mean:** They're cautious (smart)

**Response:**
> "Great question — you should be careful. We're [Company Name], local investors here in [City]. You can look us up. We never ask for money upfront, and you'd work with a real title company for closing. Happy to send you info to verify."

**Then:** Be transparent. Offer proof. Don't get defensive.

---

### "I'm already working with a realtor"

**What they mean:** They have another path

**Response:**
> "No problem at all. How's that going for you? I ask because a lot of folks come to us after trying the traditional route — our offer is usually faster and more certain, even if the number's different."

**Then:** Don't compete — compare. If they're happy with their agent, wish them well.

---

### "I've gotten other offers"

**What they mean:** They're shopping (good sign)

**Response:**
> "That's great — you should explore your options. What have those offers looked like? I want to make sure we're competitive, and if we're not the best fit, I'll tell you."

**Then:** Get intel on competition. Sometimes you'll beat them, sometimes you won't.

---

### "Why should I sell to you?"

**What they mean:** Convince me

**Response:**
> "Fair question. Three things: We close fast — usually 2-3 weeks. We buy as-is — no repairs or cleaning. And we're local — you're not dealing with some faceless company. If those things matter to you, we're a good fit. If not, I'll point you elsewhere."

**Then:** Be direct about your value prop. Confidence sells.

---

## Motivation Objections

### "I'm just hearing offers"

**What they mean:** Low motivation, shopping

**Response:**
> "Got it. What would make an offer interesting enough to actually consider? I don't want to waste your time if we're not in the right ballpark."

**Then:** Qualify harder. This is often a time-waster. Either find real motivation or move on.

---

### "I'm not really looking to sell"

**What they mean:** No urgency

**Response:**
> "No worries — I appreciate you taking my call. Just out of curiosity, is there anything happening with the property or in your life that might change that down the road?"

**Then:** Plant seeds. If nothing's there, polite exit and long-term follow-up.

---

### "I love my house, I'd never sell"

**What they mean:** Emotional attachment

**Response:**
> "Totally get it — it's your home. I'll leave you alone. But if anything ever changes — life happens — keep my number. No pressure, ever."

**Then:** Graceful exit. Don't push emotional sellers. They're not ready.

---

## Closing Objections

### "I need to sleep on it"

**What they mean:** Delay tactic or genuine processing

**Response:**
> "Of course. When you wake up tomorrow, what would make you say yes? Let's make sure I've answered everything before you think it over."

**Then:** Identify what's missing. Address it now if you can.

---

### "Send me something in writing"

**What they mean:** They want documentation (or an out)

**Response:**
> "Absolutely. What specifically would you want to see? I'll put together exactly what you need."

**Then:** Clarify expectations. Then follow through quickly.

---

### "That's my bottom line"

**What they mean:** Final offer (maybe)

**Response:**
> "I appreciate you being direct. Let me take this back and see what we can do. If we can make it work at your number, are you ready to move forward?"

**Then:** Test commitment. If yes at their number → you know your ceiling.

## The "RED" Seller

**RED = Hostile, rude, irritable**

### Signs
- Short, aggressive responses
- "That's none of your business"
- Angry tone, interrupting

### Strategy
1. **Don't match their energy** — Stay calm and professional
2. **Acknowledge** — "I hear you, and I don't want to waste your time"
3. **Find the pain** — Often they're frustrated about something else
4. **Give them an out** — "If this isn't a good time, I'm happy to call back"

### When to Walk Away
If you sense ZERO motivation underneath the hostility → polite exit.

> "I appreciate your time. Doesn't sound like we're a fit right now, but if that ever changes, we're here."

## Third-Party Story Technique

**Use when:** They have a problem and need to see you've solved it before

**Framework:**
> "We had another seller who was in the exact same situation — [describe their problem]. What we did was [your solution], and they were able to [positive outcome]. Would something like that work for you?"

**Examples:**
- Probate delays → "We've helped families through probate, started paperwork early, closed the day it cleared"
- Tenant issues → "We bought a property last month with a difficult tenant, took care of everything"
- Needed moving time → "Seller needed 90 days, we wrote it into the contract, no rush"

## Daily Practice

1. **Pick 3 objections** from this list
2. **Say your responses out loud** — alone or with a partner
3. **Role play** — Have someone throw objections at you randomly
4. **Review your calls** — Note which objections tripped you up
5. **Refine** — Adjust language to fit your voice

Objection handling is a skill. It gets better with reps.
