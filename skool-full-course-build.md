## Lesson: The Wholesaling Business Model (2-Minute Version)

Here's exactly how wholesaling works:

1. **Find a motivated seller** who needs to sell their property at a discount
2. **Get the property under contract** at a price that leaves room for profit
3. **Find a cash buyer** (usually a flipper or landlord) willing to pay more than your contract price
4. **Assign the contract** (or double close) and collect the difference

**Example:**
- Seller agrees to sell for $100,000
- You find a buyer willing to pay $115,000
- You assign the contract for a $15,000 fee
- Title company handles closing, you get paid

That's it. You never own the property. You never use your own money. You're the middleman who solves problems for both parties.

**Your value:** You find deals others can't, you move fast, and you make the transaction happen.

# MODULE 1: MARKET SELECTION

## Lesson 1.1: What Makes a Good Wholesale Market

Not all markets are equal. Here's what to look for:

**Population: 100,000+ minimum**
- You need enough transaction volume to find deals consistently
- Smaller markets = fewer opportunities = inconsistent income

**Median home price: $150K - $400K sweet spot**
- Below $150K: Margins get tight, harder to make good fees
- Above $400K: Fewer cash buyers, longer sale times
- Middle range: Plenty of buyers, healthy assignment fees

**Investor activity: Look for signs of life**
- Cash transactions as % of sales (10%+ is good)
- Presence of "We Buy Houses" signs and ads
- Active real estate investor meetups and Facebook groups
- Flipped properties on the MLS

**Job and population growth**
- People moving IN = housing demand = motivated buyers
- Check U-Haul migration data, census data, job reports

**Landlord-friendly laws (for rental buyers)**
- Eviction timeline and process
- Rent control (avoid these markets)
- Property tax rates

## Lesson 1.3: Evaluating Competition

"Is this market too saturated?" — everyone asks this. Here's the truth:

**Saturation is mostly a myth.** 
- There are enough distressed sellers in any decent-sized market
- Most wholesalers quit within 6 months
- Most wholesalers are bad at follow-up (your advantage)

**What actual competition looks like:**
- Count the "We Buy Houses" signs in the market
- Search Facebook Marketplace for wholesale deals
- Google "sell my house fast [city]" — how many PPC ads?
- Join local investor Facebook groups — how many wholesale posts?

**Healthy competition = healthy market.**
- If there's zero competition, there might be no demand
- If there's heavy competition, there's proven demand — you just need to be better

**How to compete:**
1. Faster response time (speed to lead)
2. Better follow-up (most don't follow up after day 3)
3. More offers (most don't make enough offers)
4. Better rapport (be a human, not a script robot)

## Lesson 2.2: Cost Per Lead vs. Cost Per Deal

**What actually matters:** Cost per deal, not cost per lead.

**Example:**
- Channel A: $20/lead, 2% conversion = $1,000/deal
- Channel B: $50/lead, 5% conversion = $1,000/deal
- Channel C: $100/lead, 20% conversion = $500/deal

Channel C looks expensive per lead but is cheapest per deal.

**Track these numbers from day one:**
- Leads generated
- Leads contacted
- Appointments set
- Offers made
- Contracts signed
- Deals closed
- Revenue generated

Then calculate:
- Cost per lead = Marketing spend / Leads
- Cost per deal = Marketing spend / Closed deals
- Profit per deal = Revenue - All costs

**Your goal:** Know your numbers well enough to predict revenue from marketing spend.

## Lesson 3.1: Why Leads Die

**The brutal truth:** Most leads aren't lost to competition. They're lost to poor follow-up.

**How leads die:**
- No response within 5 minutes (speed to lead)
- Only called once (most deals close after 5-12 touches)
- Left voicemail, never called back
- No system = leads fall through cracks
- "I'll call them tomorrow" = never

**The stats:**
- 50% of leads go to the vendor who responds first
- 80% of sales require 5+ follow-ups
- 44% of salespeople give up after 1 follow-up

**Your advantage:** Most of your competition is lazy. Consistent follow-up wins.

## Lesson 3.3: Pipeline Stages — From Lead to Closed

**Acquisitions Pipeline:**

1. **New Lead** — Just came in, not contacted yet
2. **Attempted Contact** — Called/texted, waiting for response
3. **Contacted** — Had a conversation, gathering info
4. **Lead Qualified** — Motivated seller, potential deal
5. **Appointment Set** — Walkthrough or offer call scheduled
6. **Offer Made** — Presented our number
7. **Under Contract** — Signed purchase agreement
8. **Closed** — Deal completed, got paid
9. **Dead** — Not a deal (archive, don't delete)

**Follow-Up Pipeline:**
1. **Hot** — Could close within 30 days
2. **Warm** — 1-3 months out
3. **Cold** — Long-term nurture (6+ months)

**Move leads through stages religiously.** Your pipeline is your business.

## Lesson 3.4: Speed to Lead

**The rule:** Respond to every inbound lead within 5 minutes.

**Why:**
- First responder gets the deal 50% of the time
- After 5 minutes, your odds drop dramatically
- Sellers are calling multiple buyers — be first

**How to achieve this:**
- Notifications on your phone for new leads
- Automation: Instant text when lead comes in ("Got your message, calling now")
- If you can't answer, have a backup (VA, partner, answering service)
- Call back missed calls within minutes, not hours

**For outbound (cold calls):**
- Speed doesn't apply the same way
- But consistency matters — call your list daily, not randomly

# MODULE 4: TALKING TO SELLERS

## Lesson 4.1: Mindset — Problem Solver, Not Salesperson

**Reframe your role:**
- You're not trying to "sell" anyone
- You're diagnosing whether you can help
- Some sellers need what you offer. Some don't.
- Your job: Find the ones you can help.

**The motivated seller:**
- Has a problem (divorce, death, foreclosure, bad tenants, job loss)
- Needs speed, convenience, certainty
- Will trade equity for those benefits

**The non-motivated seller:**
- Just "curious what they could get"
- Wants full retail price
- Has time to list traditionally

**Your job is to identify which one you're talking to — fast.**

---

## Lesson 4.2: The First Call — Opening Script

**Introduction (first 60 seconds):**

"Hey [Seller Name], this is [Your Name] with [Company]. I'm calling about the property at [Address] — you reached out about possibly selling?"

*Wait for confirmation*

**Set expectations:**

"Perfect. So I just have a few questions to see if we might be able to help. This should only take about 5-10 minutes. And honestly, if it's not a good fit for either of us, we'll just say so — no pressure, sound good?"

*Wait for agreement*

**Why this works:**
- Confirms you have the right person
- Sets a time expectation (5-10 min)
- Gives them permission to say no (reduces resistance)
- Establishes you as a professional, not a telemarketer

---

## Lesson 4.3: Building Rapport

**Rapport isn't small talk. It's earned trust.**

**How to build it:**
1. **Listen more than you talk.** Let them tell their story.
2. **Mirror their pace and energy.** Fast talker? Speed up. Slow and deliberate? Match it.
3. **Acknowledge their situation.** "That sounds really frustrating."
4. **Be human.** React naturally. Laugh when appropriate. Show empathy.

**Phrases that build rapport:**
- "Tell me more about that..."
- "How long has that been going on?"
- "That must be stressful."
- "I totally understand."

**Rapport killers:**
- Interrupting
- Rushing to the offer
- Reading a script robotically
- Not acknowledging what they said

---

## Lesson 4.4: Discovery Questions — Understanding Their Situation

**The goal:** Understand their timeline, motivation, and flexibility.

**Core questions:**

1. **Timeline:** "Ideally, when are you looking to sell by?"

2. **Motivation:** "And what's making you want to sell?"
   - Then dig deeper: "How long has that been going on?" or "Tell me more about that."

3. **Property condition:** "How would you describe the condition? Any major repairs needed?"

4. **Decision makers:** "Besides yourself, is there anyone else who needs to be involved in this decision?"

5. **Price expectations:** "Have you had the property appraised or do you have a number in mind?"
   - If they push back: "I totally understand. Most sellers I talk to have at least a ballpark — even if it's a range."

6. **Other options:** "Have you talked to any real estate agents or other investors?"

---

## Lesson 4.5: Qualifying Motivation

**Not all motivation is equal.** 

**Strong motivation (deal likely):**
- Foreclosure / pre-foreclosure
- Divorce (especially contentious)
- Inherited property (especially out of state)
- Job relocation with tight deadline
- Problem tenant / tired landlord
- Health issues / can't maintain property
- Financial distress / needs cash fast

**Weak motivation (deal unlikely):**
- "Just seeing what I can get"
- Wants full retail value
- No timeline pressure
- Lives there, happy, just curious
- "My neighbor sold for $X, I want the same"

**The test question:**

"On a scale of 1-10, where 1 is 'just curious' and 10 is 'I need to sell yesterday,' where would you say you are?"

- 1-4: Likely not a deal now. Nurture for later.
- 5-7: Possible deal. Dig deeper on motivation.
- 8-10: Hot lead. Move fast.

---

## Lesson 4.6: Handling Common Objections

**"I need to think about it."**

Response: "Totally understand. Most people do. Just so I can follow up appropriately — what specifically do you need to think through? Is it the price, the timeline, or something else?"

*This identifies the real objection.*

---

**"Your offer is too low."**

Response: "I hear you. Help me understand — what were you hoping to get?"

*Then either:*
- Justify your number with comps and repairs
- Ask what would make it work for them
- Walk away if the gap is too big

---

**"I'm talking to other investors."**

Response: "That makes total sense — you should get multiple opinions. What have they offered so far?"

*Then:*
- If you can beat it, do so
- If you can't, differentiate on speed, certainty, or service
- "We can close faster / we have our own funds / we've done 50 deals this year"

---

**"I'm going to list with an agent."**

Response: "That's definitely an option. Out of curiosity, what's making you consider going that route instead of selling directly?"

*Then address their concern:*
- If they want more money: Explain net proceeds after commissions, repairs, holding costs
- If they want exposure: Explain your buyer network
- If they don't trust investors: Build rapport, offer references

---

## Lesson 4.7: The Follow-Up Call — Re-Engaging Cold Leads

**Script for a lead you talked to 2+ weeks ago:**

"Hey [Name], this is [Your Name]. We spoke a couple weeks ago about the property on [Address]. Just wanted to check in — has anything changed with your situation?"

*Then shut up and listen.*

**Possible responses:**
- "We decided to list." → "Totally understand. If it doesn't sell, feel free to reach back out."
- "Still thinking about it." → "What would help you decide?"
- "What was your offer again?" → Repeat it, re-engage the conversation

**80% of your deals will come from follow-up, not the first call.**

# MODULE 5: MAKING OFFERS & NEGOTIATION

## Lesson 5.1: When to Make an Offer

**Don't make an offer until you have:**

1. Confirmed motivation (7+ on the 1-10 scale)
2. Understood their timeline
3. Gathered property condition info
4. Identified all decision makers
5. Gotten their price expectation (or anchored yours)

**Making an offer too early:**
- Seems desperate
- Loses negotiation leverage
- Might offer too high or too low

**The transition to offer:**

"Based on everything you've told me, I think we might be able to help. Let me run the numbers and I'll call you back with what we can do. When's a good time to connect tomorrow?"

*or, if you're ready:*

"I've got a pretty good sense of the situation. Can I share what I think we could do?"

---

## Lesson 5.2: The Offer Formula

**MAO = Maximum Allowable Offer**

**Formula:**
MAO = ARV × 70% - Repairs - Your Fee

**Example:**
- ARV (After Repair Value): $200,000
- 70% of ARV: $140,000
- Estimated repairs: $30,000
- Your wholesale fee: $10,000
- **MAO: $100,000**

**Why 70%?**
- Buyer needs 20-30% margin to profit
- Leaves room for unexpected costs
- Standard investor expectation

**Adjustments:**
- Hot market: Buyers might accept 75-80% deals
- Cold market: Might need 65% or less
- Light rehab: Can go higher on %
- Heavy rehab: Go lower (more risk)

---

## Lesson 5.3: Presenting the Offer — Anchoring

**Price anchoring:** Setting expectations before you give your number.

**Example script:**

"So I ran the numbers. Based on comparable sales in the area, after-repair value looks like about $200,000. Given the condition and what it'll need, we're looking at about $30K in repairs. Most investors in this area are buying at 65-70% of value minus repairs..."

*Pause — let them do the math*

"So what we can do is $100,000, and we can close whenever works for you — even as fast as 2 weeks if you need."

**Why this works:**
- You've justified the number before saying it
- They understand the logic
- The number doesn't feel arbitrary

---

## Lesson 5.4: Negotiation Fundamentals

**Rule 1: Never negotiate against yourself.**
- Make your offer. Wait.
- Let them counter. Don't raise your offer without them asking.

**Rule 2: Use silence.**
- After you state your offer, stop talking.
- Uncomfortable silence is powerful.
- Let them respond first.

**Rule 3: Get their number.**
- "What were you hoping to get?"
- Now you know the gap.
- Work from their number, not yours.

**Rule 4: Small concessions, slowly.**
- If you move, move small: "I could maybe do $103,000."
- Each concession should feel hard-won.

**Rule 5: Trade, don't give.**
- "I can go up to $105,000, but I'd need to close by the 15th."
- Never give without getting.

---

## Lesson 5.5: The Walk-Away

**Your most powerful tool:** Willingness to walk away.

**When to walk:**
- The gap is too big (they want $150K, you can do $100K)
- They're not motivated (just fishing for offers)
- Red flags (title issues, unrealistic expectations, dishonesty)

**How to walk away gracefully:**

"I really appreciate your time. It sounds like we're pretty far apart on price, and I don't want to waste your time. If anything changes or if you want to revisit this down the road, I'm always just a phone call away."

**Why this works:**
- No burned bridges
- They might call back in 2 weeks
- You didn't cave to a bad deal

**Many deals close AFTER you walk away.** They call back when reality sets in.

---

## Lesson 5.6: Getting the Verbal Yes

**When they agree, lock it in:**

"Awesome, so we're at $105,000, closing in 3 weeks. I'll get the paperwork drawn up and send it over today. What email should I use?"

**Then:**
- Send the contract IMMEDIATELY
- Follow up within hours to get it signed
- The longer you wait, the more likely they'll change their mind

**Speed matters.** Verbal yes + fast paperwork = signed contract.

# MODULE 6: VALUATIONS

## Lesson 6.1: ARV — After Repair Value

**ARV = What the property will be worth after repairs**

This is the most important number in your deal. Get it wrong and you lose money (or can't find a buyer).

**How to find ARV:**
1. Find 3-5 comparable sold properties (comps)
2. Adjust for differences
3. Average them out
4. Sanity check with a buyer

---

## Lesson 6.2: Finding Comps

**Where to look:**
- Zillow (free, decent)
- Redfin (free, better data)
- PropStream (paid, best)
- MLS access (if you have it)

**What makes a good comp:**
- Sold within last 6 months (3 months better)
- Within 1 mile (0.5 miles better)
- Similar size (within 20% square footage)
- Similar bed/bath count
- Similar style (ranch to ranch, not ranch to split-level)
- Similar condition AFTER repair (compare to fixed-up properties)

**Minimum 3 comps.** 5 is better.

---

## Lesson 6.3: Adjusting Comps

**No comp is perfect.** Adjust for differences:

| Factor | Adjustment |
|--------|------------|
| Square footage | +/- $50-100/sq ft |
| Bedrooms | +/- $5,000-10,000 each |
| Bathrooms | +/- $5,000-7,500 each |
| Garage | +/- $5,000-15,000 |
| Pool | +/- $10,000-20,000 |
| Lot size | Varies by market |
| Age/updates | Judgment call |

**Example:**
- Comp sold for $210,000
- Subject is 200 sq ft smaller → -$15,000
- Subject has no garage, comp does → -$10,000
- Adjusted comp value: $185,000

**Do this for all comps, then average.**

---

## Lesson 6.4: Estimating Repairs

**Quick method (for initial offers):**
- Cosmetic only (paint, carpet, cleaning): $10-15/sq ft
- Light rehab (kitchen, baths, flooring): $20-30/sq ft
- Full rehab (down to studs): $40-60/sq ft

**Example:**
- 1,500 sq ft house needs light rehab
- Estimate: 1,500 × $25 = $37,500
- Round to $35,000-40,000

**Major items to check:**
- Roof: $5,000-15,000
- HVAC: $3,000-8,000
- Foundation: $5,000-30,000+
- Electrical panel: $2,000-4,000
- Plumbing (repipe): $5,000-15,000

**Always round UP on repairs.** Surprises happen.

---

## Lesson 6.5: Verify With a Buyer

**Before you commit to a contract, sanity check with a buyer.**

Call your top 2-3 buyers:

"Hey, I've got a potential deal at [Address]. Based on my numbers, ARV looks like $185K, needs about $35K in work. What do you think you could pay for something like that?"

**This tells you:**
- If your ARV is accurate (buyers know their areas)
- What they'll actually pay
- If there's buyer demand for this deal

**Don't skip this step.** It takes 10 minutes and saves you from bad deals.

# MODULE 7: FINDING BUYERS

## Lesson 7.1: Your Buyer List is Your Business

**The truth:** A wholesaler with a strong buyer list can sell almost anything. A wholesaler without buyers is just collecting contracts they can't close.

**Your goal:** 50+ active buyers in your market.

**"Active" means:**
- They've bought in the last 6 months
- They respond to your messages
- You know their buy criteria

---

## Lesson 7.2: Where to Find Cash Buyers

**Courthouse records:**
- Pull recent cash sales (no mortgage on title)
- Skip trace the buyer's address
- Call them: "I saw you bought [Address]. Are you still buying?"

**Online:**
- BiggerPockets forums
- Facebook groups ("Real Estate Investors [City]")
- Craigslist (people posting "I Buy Houses")
- Facebook Marketplace (people looking for deals)

**Networking:**
- Local REIA meetings
- Real estate investor meetups
- Title company contacts (they know who's buying)
- Hard money lenders (they know who's borrowing)

**Your own marketing:**
- "I have a deal" posts in Facebook groups
- Craigslist ads for your deals
- Your email list

---

## Lesson 7.3: Buyer Intake — What to Collect

**When you talk to a new buyer, get:**

1. **Name and contact info** (phone, email)
2. **Entity name** (LLC they buy in)
3. **Buy box:**
   - What areas/zip codes?
   - Property types (SFR, multi, etc.)?
   - Price range?
   - Condition (turnkey, light rehab, gut job)?
4. **Speed:** How fast can they close?
5. **Volume:** How many deals/month are they looking for?
6. **Proof of funds:** Bank statement, credit line, portfolio
7. **Past purchases:** What have they bought recently?

**Store this in your CRM.** Tag buyers by criteria so you can match deals quickly.

---

## Lesson 7.4: Qualifying Buyers

**Not all buyers are equal.** Rank them:

**A-Buyers (your top 5-10):**
- Close fast (2-3 weeks)
- Make decisions quickly
- Have proof of funds
- Have closed deals before
- Don't nickel-and-dime you

**B-Buyers:**
- Close reasonably fast
- Reliable but slower on decisions
- Might need financing

**C-Buyers:**
- Tire kickers
- "Let me see 50 deals before I buy one"
- No proof of funds
- All talk, no action

**Focus your energy on A-Buyers.** Don't waste time on C-Buyers.

# MODULE 8: CLOSING THE DEAL

## Lesson 8.1: The Assignment Contract

**What it is:** You're selling your CONTRACT, not the property.

**How it works:**
1. You have a purchase agreement with the seller at $100K
2. You assign that contract to a buyer for $110K
3. Buyer pays you $10K (the assignment fee)
4. Buyer closes directly with seller

**The assignment agreement:** A simple 1-2 page document that transfers your contractual rights to the buyer.

**Key terms:**
- Assignment fee amount
- Non-refundable deposit
- Closing date
- Buyer assumes all contract terms

---

## Lesson 8.2: Double Close vs. Assignment

**Assignment:**
- Simpler, one closing
- Your fee is visible to all parties
- Some sellers/buyers don't like seeing the fee
- Lower closing costs

**Double close:**
- Two separate closings (you buy, then immediately sell)
- Your profit is private
- Higher closing costs (two transactions)
- Need transactional funding if your buyer's funds can't be used

**When to double close:**
- Large assignment fee (seller might get upset seeing $30K fee)
- Seller or buyer is sensitive about fees
- Contract doesn't allow assignment

**When to assign:**
- Small to medium fees ($5-15K)
- All parties are comfortable
- Lower closing costs

---

## Lesson 8.3: Title Company Coordination

**Your title company is your closing partner.**

**Find one that:**
- Understands wholesale deals
- Can handle assignments and double closes
- Communicates proactively
- Has competitive fees

**What they do:**
- Title search (make sure seller can sell)
- Prepare closing documents
- Hold earnest money in escrow
- Coordinate the closing
- Wire funds to all parties

**Build a relationship with your title rep.** They can save deals.

---

## Lesson 8.4: The Closing Timeline

**Typical timeline (3-4 weeks):**

**Day 1:** Contract signed with seller
**Days 2-3:** Send to title company, they begin title search
**Days 3-7:** Find and lock in your buyer
**Days 7-14:** Title work completed, clear any issues
**Days 14-21:** Schedule closing, prepare for funding
**Day 21-28:** Close, get paid

**What can delay closing:**
- Title issues (liens, judgments, probate)
- Seller doesn't have clear ownership
- Buyer's funding falls through
- Missing signatures or documents

**Stay on top of communication.** Don't assume anything is happening.

---

## Lesson 8.5: Getting Paid

**At closing:**
- Title company collects funds from buyer
- Title company pays off any liens
- Title company pays seller
- Title company pays you your assignment fee
- Everyone signs, deal records

**How you get paid:**
- Wire transfer (same day or next day)
- Check (bring to the bank)

**Document your deal:**
- Save the HUD/settlement statement
- Save the contract and assignment
- Take a photo of the check/wire confirmation
- Update your KPIs

**Celebrate.** Then get back to work.

# MODULE 9: KPIs & METRICS

## Lesson 9.1: What Gets Measured Gets Managed

**If you don't track, you can't improve.**

The difference between struggling wholesalers and successful ones: The successful ones know their numbers cold.

**Track weekly. Review weekly.** No exceptions.

---

## Lesson 9.2: Core KPIs

**Lead metrics:**
- Leads generated (total new leads)
- Leads contacted (had a conversation)
- Contact rate (contacted ÷ generated)

**Sales metrics:**
- Appointments set
- Offers made
- Contracts signed
- Deals closed

**Conversion rates:**
- Lead to contact
- Contact to appointment
- Appointment to offer
- Offer to contract
- Contract to close

**Financial metrics:**
- Marketing spend
- Cost per lead
- Cost per deal
- Average assignment fee
- Total revenue
- Net profit

---

## Lesson 9.3: Benchmarks to Aim For

**Industry averages (your mileage will vary):**

| Metric | Benchmark |
|--------|-----------|
| Lead to contact | 40-60% |
| Contact to appointment | 10-20% |
| Appointment to offer | 70-90% |
| Offer to contract | 10-20% |
| Contract to close | 70-90% |
| Overall: Leads to deals | 2-5% |

**Example:**
- 100 leads generated
- 50 contacted (50%)
- 10 appointments (20% of contacted)
- 8 offers made (80% of appointments)
- 1-2 contracts (15% of offers)
- 1-2 closed deals (90% of contracts)

**Your target:** 1-2 deals per 100 leads at minimum.

# MODULE 10: SCALING

## Lesson 10.1: When to Scale

**Scale when:**
- You're consistently closing 2+ deals/month
- Your conversion rates are stable
- Your follow-up system is working
- You have cash reserves (3+ months of expenses)
- You're turning down opportunities because you're maxed out

**Don't scale when:**
- You're not consistent yet
- Your systems are chaos
- You're scaling hope, not results

**Rule:** Don't scale chaos. Fix the foundation first.

---

## Lesson 10.2: The First Hire

**Option A: Virtual Assistant / Lead Manager**
- Handles inbound leads
- Does initial qualification
- Sets appointments for you
- Cost: $500-1,500/month (offshore) or $2,000-4,000 (US)

**Option B: Acquisition Manager**
- Handles seller calls and negotiations
- Makes offers
- Gets contracts signed
- Cost: $3,000-5,000/month base + commission

**Option C: Disposition Manager**
- Manages your buyer list
- Markets your deals
- Coordinates closings
- Cost: $2,000-4,000/month + commission

**For most people:** Start with a VA/Lead Manager. Keep acquisitions yourself until volume demands otherwise.

---

## Lesson 10.3: Documenting Your Processes (SOPs)

**SOP = Standard Operating Procedure**

**Before you hire, document everything:**
- How to answer an inbound lead
- How to qualify a seller
- How to make an offer
- How to send a contract
- How to coordinate closing

**Format:**
1. Title: What this process is
2. Trigger: When to do it
3. Steps: Exactly what to do (numbered)
4. Tools: What systems to use
5. Examples: Screenshots, scripts, templates

**A good SOP means anyone can follow it.** If you get hit by a bus, the business keeps running.

---

## Lesson 10.4: Training Team Members

**Training process:**
1. **Show them:** Do the task while they watch
2. **Explain why:** Not just what, but why it matters
3. **Let them try:** Observe while they do it
4. **Give feedback:** Correct mistakes immediately
5. **Let them fly:** Check in periodically

**Use call recordings.** Have them listen to your calls. Grade their calls using Gunner or similar.

**First 2 weeks:** They shadow you
**Weeks 3-4:** They do it, you review everything
**Month 2+:** They're independent, you spot-check

---

## Lesson 10.5: Compensation Structures

**Lead Manager:**
- Flat hourly or monthly rate
- Optional: Bonus per qualified lead or appointment set

**Acquisition Manager:**
- Base: $3,000-5,000/month
- Commission: 10-20% of assignment fee on their deals

**Disposition Manager:**
- Base: $2,000-3,500/month
- Commission: 5-10% of deals they close

**Align incentives.** Pay for results, not just activity.

---

## Lesson 10.6: Adding Markets

**When you're ready to expand geographically:**

1. Pick a market using Module 1 criteria
2. Build a small buyer list FIRST (10-20 buyers)
3. Validate demand (can you move deals there?)
4. Launch lead gen
5. Hire locally or manage virtually

**Virtual wholesaling works** but requires stronger systems and more trust.

**Start with one new market.** Prove it works before adding another.

---

## Lesson 10.7: The Owner's Role

**As you scale, your role changes:**

**Solo operator:**
- You do everything
- Revenue tied to your time

**Small team (1-3 people):**
- You train and manage
- You handle complex deals
- You focus on acquisitions or dispositions (your strength)

**Real business (4+ people):**
- You manage managers
- You focus on strategy, marketing, growth
- You're replaceable in daily operations

**Goal:** Build a business that runs without you. That's where freedom lives.
