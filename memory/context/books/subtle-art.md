# The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck
**Author:** Mark Manson | **Processed:** 2026-03-15

---

## Core Thesis
The key to a good life is not caring about more things — it's caring about *fewer, better* things. You have a limited supply of fucks to give. The question isn't how to solve all your problems; it's how to choose problems worth having. Suffering is inevitable. The only choice is *what* you'll suffer for.

---

## Key Mental Models

### 1. The Feedback Loop from Hell
Caring too much about a problem makes you anxious about the anxiety, guilty about the guilt, ashamed of the shame. The solution is not to feel better — it's to care less about the fact that you feel bad.

### 2. Values Determine Problems
Your problems reflect your values. If you value appearing successful, you'll always be anxious about perception. If you value building something real, your problems become: "Is this actually good?" That's a more useful problem.

### 3. Pain Is Inevitable / Suffering Is Optional
You will have hard days, losing deals, bad hires, failed product launches. That's not the problem. The problem is when you treat unavoidable pain as a signal that something has gone fundamentally wrong with your life.

### 4. Responsibility vs. Fault
You didn't cause the market to shift. You didn't cause that team member to be lazy. But you are responsible for how you respond. Fault is past-facing; responsibility is forward-facing.

### 5. The "Do Something" Principle
Motivation doesn't precede action — action precedes motivation. Don't wait to feel ready or inspired. Do something, however small. The action creates the clarity.

## Application to NAH

**Lead/Deal Selection:**
Not every lead deserves Corey's personal attention. Not every deal is worth the energy. The most dangerous thing is chasing volume and filling the funnel with noise. Fewer, higher-quality fucks = fewer, better deals pursued deeply.

**Delegation as Value Alignment:**
Every task Corey does himself that someone else could do is a misallocated fuck. Systematizing NAH isn't about working less — it's about choosing the problems that only Corey can solve and offloading everything else.

**Team Problems:**
Some team friction is just the cost of operating. Not every Kyle or Daniel problem requires Corey's intervention. Tolerating the right level of messiness is a feature of a real operation, not a bug.

---

## Application to Gunner

**Product Focus:**
The most important product decisions are the ones about what *not* to build. Every feature request is someone else's fuck being placed on the Gunner roadmap. The discipline is in choosing which problems Gunner was *built* to solve, and declining the rest.

**Market Positioning:**
Gunner can't be everything to everyone. Pick the customer you're genuinely trying to help and suffer for *that* problem deeply. Shallow coverage of many use cases = weak value for all.

**Metrics:**
Not all metrics are worth caring about. Vanity metrics (signups, impressions) vs. metrics tied to actual user success. Manson's filter: does this metric tell you whether users are actually getting better at their jobs?

## Behavioral Rules for Xhaka

1. **When Corey is stressed or overwhelmed**, apply the "limited fucks" filter: *Is this actually worth one of your limited fucks today? What happens if you don't solve this today?* Help him triage by what genuinely matters.

2. **When Corey is chasing a deal or opportunity that smells like distraction**, ask: *Does this align with the values/goals NAH is actually optimizing for? Or is this just interesting?*

3. **When Corey is waiting to take action on something "until it feels right"**, invoke the Do Something principle: *What's the smallest possible action you could take right now?*

4. **When Gunner feature prioritization comes up**, apply the Manson filter: *Which of these features addresses the problem Gunner was actually built to solve? Which is someone else's fuck being added to the roadmap?*

5. **When Corey blames external circumstances** (market, a seller, a team member), acknowledge the fault vs. responsibility split: *You didn't cause this. What's the one thing you're responsible for doing differently from here?*
