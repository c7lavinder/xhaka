# The E-Myth Revisited — Michael Gerber

> "The fatal assumption: if you understand the technical work of a business, you understand a business that does that technical work."

---

## Core Thesis

Most small businesses fail because they are built by **Technicians** — people who are great at doing the work, but not at running a business that does the work. Gerber identifies three personalities that must coexist in every successful entrepreneur:

- **The Technician** — loves doing the work; lives in the present; "if you want it done right, do it yourself"
- **The Manager** — loves order and process; creates systems; lives in the past (building on what worked)
- **The Entrepreneur** — lives in the future; sees opportunity; works ON the business, not IN it

The trap: most founders are predominantly Technicians. When business grows, they get crushed by volume and can never escape — because they never built the business to run without them.

---

## Key Mental Models

### 1. The Franchise Prototype
Every system in your business should be built as if you're going to franchise it — i.e., a complete stranger should be able to run it successfully with the manual. McDonald's isn't successful because its food is exceptional; it's successful because its **system** is exceptional and replicable.

**Implication:** Document every process. If it lives in someone's head (especially Corey's), it's a liability.

### 2. Systems vs People
Don't build a business that depends on exceptional people. Build a system that ordinary, well-trained people can operate consistently. People will leave; systems persist.

**Implication:** When something goes wrong at NAH — don't ask "who failed?" Ask "what system failed or was missing?"

### 3. Working ON vs IN the Business
- **IN the business:** doing deals, taking calls, solving fires, being the closer
- **ON the business:** designing the system that finds, qualifies, and closes deals without you

Corey's highest-leverage activity is always working ON. Every hour Corey spends IN is an hour the business doesn't grow.

### 4. The Turn-Key Revolution
The goal of business isn't to create a job for yourself — it's to create a **turn-key system** that produces results without you. You sell/exit/scale the system, not yourself.

## Application to NAH (New Again Houses)

**Current state:** Corey is frequently the Technician. He is the knowledge base, the closer, the quality check, the fallback. When he's not available, things stall.

**What needs to happen:**
- Kyle, Chris, and Daniel should operate from a documented playbook — not from asking Corey
- Every acquisition decision should have a system (GHL pipeline, comp logic, offer formula) — not require Corey's gut
- Corey's job is to tune the system and remove himself from the daily flow, not to be the best player on the field

**Specific risks:**
- If Corey is the only one who can value a property → single point of failure
- If the team escalates decisions to Corey by default → the system never learns to operate alone
- If onboarding a new AM means Corey has to train them personally → no franchise prototype exists

---

## Application to Gunner

- Gunner must work without Corey doing demos, closing accounts, or coaching customers
- The product itself needs to be the Technician — the AI does the grading, the system does the coaching
- Sales must be systematized: onboarding flows, pitch decks, trial sequences that convert without Corey
- Every feature Corey manually provides to a customer = a feature that needs to be built into the product

## Behavioral Rules for Xhaka

1. **When Corey describes himself doing something that a system should do → flag it.** Say: "That's a Technician task. Who or what should own that system?"

2. **When diagnosing a NAH breakdown → ask "what system was missing?" not "who made the mistake?"** Focus on the process gap, not the person.

3. **When Corey proposes a solution that requires him specifically → push back.** Ask: "How would this work if you were out of the country for 2 weeks?"

4. **Regularly ask the ON/IN question:** "How much of your week was working IN the business vs ON it?" If the ratio is bad, flag it.

5. **When reviewing team performance → check if the failure is a training/system gap, not a talent gap.** Most underperformance is a missing system, not a bad hire.
