# The E-Myth Revisited — Michael Gerber

## Core Thesis
Most businesses are started by Technicians having an entrepreneurial seizure. They know how to do the work, but don't know how to build a business that does the work. The fatal assumption: if you understand the technical work of a business, you understand the business that does that work.

## Key Mental Models

### The Three Personalities
- **Technician** — lives in the present, does the work, hates being managed. "If you want it done right, do it yourself."
- **Manager** — lives in the past, creates order, manages the Technician. Pragmatic.
- **Entrepreneur** — lives in the future, creates vision, drives change. Most founders have too little of this in the actual building phase.

### The Franchise Prototype
Build every process as if you're going to franchise it. Document everything. The system runs the business; people run the system. McDonald's doesn't depend on a great chef — it depends on a great system.

### Working ON vs IN the Business
Working IN: doing the technical work (wholesaling deals, coaching calls)
Working ON: building the system that does the work without you

### The Turn-Key Revolution
The goal isn't a great product — it's a great business that delivers a great product consistently, without depending on any one person.

## Application to NAH
- Corey is the Technician trap. His goal ($300k/mo running without him) is literally the E-Myth solution.
- Every process Kyle, Daniel, Chris run should be documented as if training a franchise location
- When Corey is the only one who knows how to handle something → that's a system gap, not a staffing gap
- The GHL pipeline, Gunner coaching, KPI entry — all should be franchise-ready documented systems

## Application to Gunner
- Gunner itself is the franchise prototype for sales coaching — it removes the dependency on one great manager
- The product must work without Corey demoing or explaining it (turn-key)
- Every feature should ask: does this work when Corey isn't in the room?

## Behavioral Rules for Xhaka

- When Gunner features are being designed, apply the franchise prototype test: does this work without explanation?
- When a process breaks and the fix is "Corey does it" → that's not a fix, that's a symptom
