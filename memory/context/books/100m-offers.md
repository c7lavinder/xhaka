# $100M Offers — Alex Hormozi

> "Make people an offer so good they would feel stupid saying no."

---

## Core Thesis

Most businesses compete on price because their offers are commodities. Hormozi argues the solution isn't better marketing — it's a **Grand Slam Offer** so differentiated and value-stacked that price becomes irrelevant.

The **Value Equation:**

```
Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Achievement)
        / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice)
```

To increase value: maximize the top (bigger dream, more believable) and minimize the bottom (faster, easier). Price is only painful when value is unclear.

---

## Key Mental Models

### 1. The Grand Slam Offer
Not a product. Not a service. A complete **outcome** with every objection pre-handled. It bundles:
- The core result they want
- Bonuses that eliminate excuses
- Guarantees that remove risk
- A delivery mechanism that feels fast and easy

**Implication:** Stop selling a service. Start selling a specific, believable transformation.

### 2. Niche Down to Win
The riches are in the niches. A generic offer for everyone converts poorly at low price. A hyper-specific offer for a specific avatar converts at high price. The more specific the promise, the more believable it is.

**Formula:** Identify the ONE customer, ONE pain, ONE outcome, ONE timeline.

### 3. Price vs Value
Charging premium prices isn't just acceptable — it's necessary:
- Higher price = higher perceived value (psychology)
- Higher price = better clients (skin in the game)
- Higher price = more resources to deliver results
- "Selling to the price-conscious is a trap"

Competing on price is a race to the bottom. The goal is to be so clearly different that price comparison is impossible.

### 4. Bonuses and Guarantees
- **Bonuses:** Add items with high perceived value / low cost to deliver. Each bonus should eliminate a specific objection.
- **Guarantees:** Reverse the risk entirely. "If you don't get X result in Y days, you get Z." The confidence to offer a guarantee comes from understanding your conversion rates.

### 5. The Scarcity/Urgency Stack
Scarcity (limited supply) + Urgency (time limit) + Bonuses (stack value) + Guarantee (remove risk) = irresistible offer. These aren't manipulation — they're clarity.

## Application to NAH (New Again Houses)

**Offer improvements using Hormozi's lens:**
- **Dream outcome for motivated sellers:** Fast cash, zero hassle, no repairs, certain close
- **Perceived likelihood:** Testimonials, proof of past closings, track record, local credibility
- **Time delay:** Close in 7 days vs 30-90 days with a realtor — this is huge value
- **Effort & sacrifice:** No showings, no repairs, no commissions, no uncertainty

**The NAH Grand Slam pitch:** "We buy your house as-is, close in [X] days, no fees, no repairs, guaranteed offer — or we pay you $[X]."

**What's missing:** A clearly communicated guarantee that removes seller doubt. If NAH guarantees the offer won't drop after inspection (on qualified properties), that's a differentiator.

## Application to Gunner

**Hormozi reframe for Gunner's pitch:**
- **Dream outcome:** "Your team closes 20% more deals without you being on every call"
- **Perceived likelihood:** Show proof — grade 5 calls for free, show what changed, show results
- **Time delay:** "See your team's improvement in 30 days or it's free"
- **Effort & sacrifice:** "Set up in 10 minutes. No extra work from your team."

**Gunner Grand Slam Offer:** "We'll grade your team's last 10 calls, identify the top 3 patterns killing your close rate, and show you a specific script fix — in 48 hours, for free. If you don't see at least one thing you can change immediately, we'll send you $50."

**Pricing:** Don't race to bottom. Charge for outcomes. Position as revenue-generating, not a cost.

## Behavioral Rules for Xhaka

1. **When Corey describes a new offer or pitch → run it through the Value Equation.** "What's the dream outcome? How believable is it? How fast? How easy?" If any quadrant is weak, flag it.

2. **When NAH conversion rates are low → first ask about the offer, not the leads.** Most conversion problems are offer problems, not traffic problems.

3. **When Corey is thinking about price → push premium.** Default recommendation is to charge more, not less, and stack more value.

4. **When designing Gunner features or pricing → ask "what objection does this eliminate?"** Every bonus, feature, and tier should exist to remove a specific reason NOT to buy.

5. **When Corey is frustrated that a campaign isn't working → before changing targeting, ask: "Is the offer a Grand Slam? Would YOU feel stupid saying no to it?"**
