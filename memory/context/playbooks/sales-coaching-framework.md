# Sales Coaching Framework — NAH Team Development System

This document defines how Corey coaches the NAH sales team — not just what to look for, but how to structure feedback, build habits, and use Gunner scores as a performance lever rather than a report card.

---

## The Core Principle: Coaching Changes Identity, Not Just Skills

The difference between reps who improve and reps who plateau is identity.

A rep who sees themselves as "someone who struggles with objections" will find ways to confirm that belief. A rep who sees themselves as "someone who's getting better at objections every week" will find evidence to confirm that instead.

**Skill-based coaching:** "Here's what you did wrong. Here's what to do instead."
**Identity-based coaching:** "Here's the pattern I see in your calls. Is that who you want to be as a rep?"

Skill coaching changes behavior. Identity coaching changes direction. The best reps need both.

The Atomic Habits principle applies: **every coaching interaction is either a vote for the rep's improved identity or a vote against it.** Criticism without direction is a vote against. A rep leaving every coaching session with one concrete practice to do differently is a vote for.

## 1. Call Review Methodology

### What to Look For (Gunner Score Dimensions)
Gunner scores calls across multiple dimensions. The coaching value is in *which* dimensions are dragging the score, not the score itself.

**High-signal dimensions to analyze:**
- **Rapport Building** — Did the rep connect before converting? Sellers buy from people they like.
- **Discovery Quality** — Did the rep uncover the *why behind the sale*, or just the what?
- **Objection Handling** — Did they follow cushion → reverse → truth-tell, or panic and fold?
- **Next Steps Commitment** — Did the call end with a specific next action, date, and time?
- **Talking Ratio** — Are they listening 60%+ of the call, or pitching monologues?
- **Tone and Energy** — Monotone = disengaged seller. Genuine enthusiasm is audible.

### Call Selection Strategy
Don't just review the worst calls. Review a balanced mix:
- 1 low-scoring call (diagnose failure modes)
- 1 high-scoring call (identify what's working, reinforce it)
- 1 call the rep specifically flagged as "I'm not sure what happened here"

Reviewing only failures creates learned helplessness. Reviewing only wins creates blind spots.

### How to Give Effective Feedback

**The 3-2-1 Frame:**
- 3 specific moments they did well (exact timestamps, exact language)
- 2 moments that cost them (specific point in the call, what happened, what should have happened)
- 1 drill to practice before the next review

**What "specific" looks like:**
❌ "Your rapport-building was weak."
✅ "At 2:14, when the seller mentioned their daughter is in the hospital, you said 'okay let's talk about the property.' That's the moment you lost her. The right move was to stay with the daughter for 60 seconds before transitioning. Let's practice that transition."

**The 24-48 hour rule:** Feedback delivered more than 48 hours after a call is significantly less effective. Gunner surfaces calls quickly — use them the same day or next day.

## 2. Score-Based Coaching — Using Gunner Scores Actionably

### What Scores Tell You (and What They Don't)

A Gunner score is a **diagnostic signal**, not a verdict. A 70% rep and a 36% rep aren't "good" and "bad" — they're at different parts of the development curve, with different gaps.

**Score ranges and what they mean:**
| Score | Status | Coaching Approach |
|---|---|---|
| 75%+ | High performer | Optimization mode — work on advanced techniques, edge cases |
| 65–74% | Solid | Identify 1-2 specific weaknesses to target; mostly positive reinforcement |
| 55–64% | Developing | Weekly structured coaching; drilling on 1 skill at a time |
| 45–54% | Struggling | 2x/week coaching; fundamentals focus; identity work needed |
| <45% | Crisis | Daily check-ins; structured drill program; consider role fit |

### Score Trend Analysis
A single score is a data point. A trend is a story.

- **Improving trend:** Reinforce what changed. Ask the rep what they did differently.
- **Flat trend:** The current coaching approach isn't working. Change the method, not the message.
- **Declining trend:** Burnout or complacency — address the energy/motivation layer before skills.
- **Volatile scores (high variation):** The rep has the skill but not the habit. Focus on consistency over peaks.

### Benchmarks for NAH
- **LM minimum acceptable:** 65%
- **AM minimum acceptable:** 65%
- **LG minimum acceptable:** 55%
- **Current benchmarks:** Kyle 70% (AM), Daniel 67% (LM)
- **Priority coaching cases:** Chris 36% (LM), Efren 48% (LG), Mirna 43% (LG)

## 3. How to Coach Chris (36%) vs. Kyle (70%)

These two reps need fundamentally different coaching approaches.

### Coaching Chris (36% — Priority 1)

**The situation:** Chris is at a critical inflection point. Reps at 36% either turn a corner in 30-60 days or self-select out. The coaching relationship is as important as the content.

**What NOT to do with Chris:**
- Don't review 3 bad calls in a row. It's demoralizing and creates shame spirals.
- Don't give him a laundry list of things to fix. He'll try to fix everything and fix nothing.
- Don't compare him to Daniel or Kyle explicitly. Implicit comparison is fine; explicit is deflating.

**What TO do:**
1. **Identity reset first.** Start with: "Chris, what kind of sales rep do you want to be in 6 months?" Get his answer. Anchor coaching to his vision, not your standard.

2. **One skill per week.** This week: only work on the opening. Next week: only work on discovery. The week after: objection handling. Don't stack.

3. **Find the one thing he does well.** Even at 36%, there's something. Maybe his energy is good. Maybe he's great at empathy. Name it, make it his identity anchor: "You're naturally good at making sellers feel heard — that's your superpower. Let's build the technical skills around that."

4. **Daily micro-drills.** 10 minutes before his shift: role-play one scenario. Specifically the one where he tends to fail. Repetition > variety at this stage.

5. **Weekly score review together.** Chris should see his score improving in real time. Even 36% → 42% is worth celebrating. "You added 6 points this week. That's real."

**Gunner-specific approach for Chris:**
Look specifically at what's dragging him to 36%. Is it one dimension or many? If one dimension (e.g., objection handling) is pulling him down, fix that first and his overall score jumps. Pick the highest-weight, lowest-performing dimension.

**30-day target:** 50%+
**60-day target:** 60%+
**90-day target:** 65%+

If not at 65% by 90 days with consistent coaching, have a frank conversation about role fit.

---

### Coaching Kyle (70% — Optimization)

**The situation:** Kyle is the benchmark. Coaching at this level is about edge cases, not fundamentals. The trap is coaching him the same way as Chris — he'll disengage.

**What Kyle needs:**
1. **Advanced scenario drilling.** The easy objections aren't his gap. Run him through the hard ones: sellers with $50K in equity but a competing cash buyer, multi-family with title complications, emotional seller who's been scammed before.

2. **Watch ratio on his calls.** At 70%, the next 5 points usually come from listening more and pitching less. Pull his talk/listen ratio from calls. If he's above 50% talking, that's the target.

3. **Consistency over peaks.** If his score variance is high (some 80% calls, some 55%), he has the skill but not the habit. The coaching goal is narrowing the spread — fewer 55% days, more 70%+ days.

4. **Give him a coaching role.** Having Kyle do one peer-coaching call with Chris or Daniel per week does two things: (a) forces Kyle to articulate what he does well (ingrains it), (b) creates team accountability culture. He owns the outcome of the team.

5. **Challenge his ceiling.** "You're at 70%. The top AMs in the industry are at 80%+. What would you need to change to get there?"

**70% → 80% target:** Not a 30-day goal. This is a 90-day horizon. The improvements at the top end are finer and slower.

## 4. The 1% Improvement Loop

Borrowed from Atomic Habits: improving 1% every day creates a 37x improvement over a year. For sales reps, this means:

**Every call should produce:**
- 1 thing the rep noticed themselves doing well
- 1 thing they'd do differently
- 1 adjustment to try on the next call

This doesn't require a manager — it's a self-coaching habit. Build it into the team culture via Gunner: after every call, reps log a 30-second audio note before the score comes in. "What I did well. What I'd change."

### Making It Structural (Not Just Motivational)

The 1% loop only works if it's systematic:

1. **Pre-call intention:** "What's the one skill I'm specifically practicing on this call?"
2. **Post-call reflection:** (30 seconds) "Did I practice it? What happened?"
3. **Weekly tracking:** Score trend + skills practiced chart. Not just the number, but what changed.

This turns coaching from a correction cycle into a growth identity.

## 5. The Weekly Coaching Cadence

### Standard Weekly Rhythm

| Day | Activity | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Team score review — flag the week's priority coaching cases | 15 min |
| Tuesday | 1:1 with priority rep (Chris first) — call review + drill | 30 min |
| Wednesday | Role-play drill with that rep — apply Tuesday's lesson | 20 min |
| Thursday | Secondary rep coaching (Daniel, Kyle, LGs) | 20-30 min each |
| Friday | Score check — did the week's coaching show up in calls? | 10 min |

**Corey's actual time commitment:** ~2 hours/week if structured. Without structure, it becomes 5+ hours of reactive firefighting.

### Monthly Cadence

- **Week 1:** Focus on discovery skills across the team
- **Week 2:** Focus on objection handling (rotate through objection types)
- **Week 3:** Focus on closing and next-steps commitment
- **Week 4:** Scenario variety — unexpected situations, hard sellers, edge cases

Rotating the monthly focus keeps coaching fresh and ensures all dimensions get attention.

## 6. Coaching vs. Managing

**Managing:** Telling people what to do.
**Coaching:** Helping people understand why and develop their own judgment.

The distinction matters because managed reps need you to function. Coached reps function without you.

Signs you're managing instead of coaching:
- You tell them what to say vs. asking what they'd say differently
- Your feedback is always about what they did wrong vs. what question led there
- Reps come to you with every objection vs. handling it themselves

The test: if Corey is unavailable for a week, do scores go up or down? If they go down, the team is managed. If they hold or improve, they're coached.

## 7. Gunner Integration Points

### What Gunner Gives You
- **Leaderboard:** Real-time score visibility creates healthy competition
- **XP system:** Gamification rewards activity + quality, not just outcomes
- **Call library:** Filter by score, by dimension, by rep — build a library of best calls for training
- **Trend data:** Week-over-week improvement tracking per rep

### What Gunner Doesn't Give You
- Context (why a call went that way)
- Rep effort and mindset
- Relationship quality with the seller

Use Gunner for the *what*, coaching conversations for the *why*.

### Best Practice: Build a "Hall of Fame" Call Library
Tag the top 10 calls per role in Gunner as "Hall of Fame." When a new LM or AM joins, their first week of training is listening to those 10 calls, not a manual. Real calls teach better than scripts.
