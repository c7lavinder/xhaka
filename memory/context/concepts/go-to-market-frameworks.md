# Go-To-Market Frameworks — Strategy for Gunner & NAH

Go-to-market (GTM) strategy answers one deceptively simple question: **how does your product find the right customers, and how do they find you?** Get this wrong and you build a product nobody buys. Get it right and distribution becomes the moat. This file synthesizes the most important GTM frameworks and applies them directly to Gunner (AI sales coaching SaaS) and NAH (wholesale real estate acquisition).

---

## 1. Product-Led Growth (PLG) vs. Sales-Led Growth (SLG)

These are the two fundamental GTM archetypes. They determine your entire org structure, sales motion, and capital requirements.

### Product-Led Growth (PLG)
The product is the primary vehicle for acquisition, activation, and expansion. Users discover value before talking to a salesperson — often before they pay anything.

**Signature characteristics:**
- Free tier or free trial that delivers real value
- Viral or word-of-mouth spreading inside organizations
- Self-serve onboarding — no human required to get started
- Expansion driven by usage (more seats, more volume, more features)
- Classic examples: Slack, Figma, Notion, Linear, Calendly

**Works when:**
- The product is intuitive enough for a buyer to evaluate without hand-holding
- The end-user and the buyer are the same person (or the end-user can pull the buyer in)
- Time-to-value is under 10 minutes
- The product delivers an "aha moment" that makes the value undeniable

### Sales-Led Growth (SLG)
Sales reps are the primary acquisition engine. The product is demonstrated, not self-explored. Deals are won through relationships and proposals, not product trials.

**Works when:**
- The product requires configuration or data import to show value
- The buyer and the end-user are different (manager buys, team uses)
- The contract value is high enough to justify human CAC
- The buying decision involves multiple stakeholders

### Which fits Gunner?

**Gunner is currently SLG with PLG potential.**

*Why SLG now:*
- Gunner connects to GHL — there's setup friction that requires onboarding
- The buyer (team owner/operator) is not the end-user (LMs making calls)
- The value is only apparent once real calls are flowing and being graded
- The ICP (wholesale RE operators) is not a self-serve software buyer by default

*PLG potential later:*
- Once the GHL integration is seamless (sub-5-min setup), a free trial with real calls becomes viable
- Social proof within wholesale operator networks is powerful — one user showing another the leaderboard during a mastermind call is organic PLG
- The leaderboard feature is inherently viral within teams (end-users compete, manager sees it working, expands seats)

**Near-term recommendation:** Lean SLG. Personal outreach, demos, onboarding calls. Build the PLG infrastructure (self-serve signup, in-product onboarding) as a Phase 2 motion once there are 25+ customers proving the model.

## 2. Bottom-Up vs. Top-Down Enterprise Entry

Even within SLG, there's a strategic choice: do you enter through the economic buyer (top-down) or through the end-user who then pulls in budget (bottom-up)?

**Top-Down:** You sell to the operator/owner first. They mandate the tool for the team. Fast to close if you can reach the right person. Fragile if the champion leaves.

**Bottom-Up:** An LM or team member discovers the tool, loves it, advocates for it internally. Slower but creates genuine adoption. Stickier long-term.

**For Gunner:** Pure top-down is the right motion at this stage. Wholesale RE operators are the ICP. LMs aren't buying software. The operator wants accountability and coaching leverage — Gunner solves their problem. Sell to operators, configure for LMs.

**Warning:** Don't confuse "end-user delight" with "buyer access." Build features LMs love (the leaderboard, the feedback cards), but sell to operators. The LM experience reduces churn after the sale; it doesn't generate the sale.

## 3. Crossing the Chasm — Geoffrey Moore

Moore's framework describes the gap between early adopters and the mainstream market. Most products die in "the chasm" between these two groups.

**The Technology Adoption Lifecycle:**
1. **Innovators** (2.5%) — Tech enthusiasts. Buy because it's new. High tolerance for bugs.
2. **Early Adopters** (13.5%) — Visionaries. Buy to get competitive advantage. Will tolerate pain for upside.
3. **Early Majority** (34%) — Pragmatists. Buy when the risk is gone. Want whole products, references, ROI proof.
4. **Late Majority** (34%) — Conservatives. Buy when it's standard.
5. **Laggards** (16%) — Skeptics. Last to adopt.

**The Chasm:** Between early adopters and early majority. Early adopters accept incomplete products. Early majority won't buy without a proven use case, clear ROI, and peer validation.

**Gunner's current position:** Early adopters. The first 10–20 customers are visionary wholesale operators who see the potential and are willing to be part of building something. Corey is one. This is the *right* group to start with — they'll give feedback, tolerate bugs, and become case studies.

**Crossing the Chasm for Gunner:**
- Define a beachhead: "Wholesale RE teams of 3–8 doing 10+ deals/year"
- Build a whole product for that beachhead: GHL integration + leaderboard + weekly call review + onboarding call
- Collect 5 undeniable case studies: "Team X closed Y more deals. Operator saved Z hours/week."
- Use those 5 to access the early majority: conferences, mastermind groups, referrals
- The early majority wants to hear from peers, not vendors. Your early adopters are your salespeople.

**NAH parallel:** NAH's marketing already crossed this chasm in the direct mail/PPL space — those channels are proven. The chasm question for NAH is whether new channels (BatchDialer, AI-assisted follow-up) can reach motivated sellers at scale before competitors.

## 4. Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)

Clayton Christensen's framework reframes why customers buy: **people don't buy products; they hire them to do a job.**

The job has three dimensions:
- **Functional job:** The practical task it accomplishes
- **Emotional job:** How it makes the buyer feel
- **Social job:** How it makes them look to others

### What job does Gunner get hired to do?

**Functional job:** "Help me see what's happening on my team's calls without spending 10 hours a week listening to recordings. Tell me who's improving, who's stuck, and what to coach."

**Emotional job:** "Give me confidence that I'm not missing something. Reduce the anxiety of running a team where I can't be in every conversation."

**Social job:** "Help me be the operator who has their team dialed in — who can walk into a mastermind and say my LMs have a 72% contact-to-appointment rate."

**The competitive insight from JTBD:** Gunner isn't competing with other AI coaching tools. It's competing with *doing nothing*, with *spreadsheets and manual call reviews*, and with *trusting that things are fine*. The real competitor is inertia. Sell against inertia, not against software.

### What job does a NAH seller hire New Again Houses to do?

**Functional job:** "Get me out of this property quickly, at a known price, with minimal hassle."

**Emotional job:** "Relieve the stress of owning a problem I can't fix. Make me feel like I made a smart decision."

**Social job:** "Let me tell my spouse/family that I handled this. Not a burden anymore."

**JTBD implication for NAH:** The competitor isn't other wholesalers or even listing agents. It's the seller's **resistance to deciding**. The functional job is clear; the emotional job is what drives a call to action. Scripts should address emotional relief, not just price logic.

## 5. Niche-First Expansion

The single biggest GTM mistake operators make: trying to be relevant to everyone before being dominant with someone.

**The Niche-First Principle:** Own a vertical completely before expanding. Being the best at one thing is always more valuable than being okay at many.

**Why this works:**
- Word-of-mouth is community-specific. If Gunner is "the tool wholesale RE teams use," every operator who hears about it from another operator already has context. That's zero cold education required.
- You can build vertical-specific features (wholesale-specific call frameworks, MAO conversation scoring, motivated seller language detection) that generic tools can't match.
- You become the default. Defaults are impossible to displace.

**Gunner's niche:** Wholesale real estate operators. Not "sales teams." Not "real estate." Not "call coaching." Wholesale RE operators with lead managers making outbound and inbound calls. This is the beachhead.

**Expansion path (when ready):**
1. Wholesale RE → Adjacent RE acquisition models (fix-and-flip teams, land investors)
2. RE broadly → High-volume outbound service businesses (HVAC, roofing, solar door-to-door)
3. Broad sales coaching for high-repetition outbound teams

Each expansion uses the credibility and case studies from the prior vertical. You don't abandon wholesale RE — you dominate it and use that credibility as a wedge into adjacent markets.

## 6. ICP — Ideal Customer Profile for Gunner

An ICP is not a persona. It's a precise definition of the company/individual most likely to buy, stay, and expand. Every GTM decision flows from ICP clarity.

### Gunner's ICP (Primary)

**Company characteristics:**
- Business type: Wholesale real estate operation (buy low, assign/flip to investors)
- Team size: 3–10 people, including 2+ LMs making calls daily
- Deal volume: 5–20 deals/month (or aspiring to reach that level)
- Tech stack: GHL (or willing to adopt it)
- Revenue: $500K–$5M/year in wholesale margin
- Markets: Single market or multi-market expansion phase

**Economic buyer characteristics:**
- Role: Operator/Founder/Managing Partner
- Pain: Can't scale because they're the bottleneck in coaching and QA
- Motivation: Wants to remove themselves from daily call review without losing accountability
- Sophistication: Attends masterminds, follows RE influencers, invests in team development
- Buying behavior: Makes decisions quickly if trust is established; peer validation matters

**Negative ICP (do not pursue):**
- Solo wholesalers with no team (no team = no coaching value)
- Traditional agents or brokers (different call frameworks entirely)
- Operators who haven't started making calls systematically (they need a different tool first)
- PE-backed large iBuyers (procurement cycle, wrong motion)

### NAH Seller ICP

**Primary:** Homeowner in Nashville metro with a distressed property (deferred maintenance, inherited, behind on payments, divorce, estate). Motivated by timeline, not maximum price. Has some equity.

**Negative:** Homeowners testing the market who just want a price check. Sellers without equity. Retail-ready properties (agent is the right tool for them).

## 7. Channel Strategy — Which Channels to Own First

Channel selection is a forced choice. You can't execute 5 channels simultaneously at startup scale. Pick one, dominate it, then expand.

### The Channel Scoring Framework

For each potential channel, rate 1–5 on:
- **Reach:** How many ICPs can this channel access?
- **CAC:** What does it cost to acquire a customer through this channel?
- **Fit:** How naturally does this channel fit how our ICP discovers solutions?
- **Control:** Do we own this channel or are we at someone else's mercy?
- **Speed:** How fast can this channel generate results?

### Gunner Channel Analysis

**Direct outreach (DM/email to wholesale operators):**
- Reach: Medium (operator list is findable — masterminds, FB groups, GHL users)
- CAC: Low (sweat equity)
- Fit: High (operators are accessible, respond to peer recommendations)
- Control: High
- Speed: Fast — first customers within weeks

**Wholesale mastermind partnerships:**
- Reach: Medium-High (a well-placed intro at a 200-person event > 200 cold emails)
- CAC: Low-Medium (speaking slot, sponsorship, or partner deal)
- Fit: Very High — operators already in buying mode at masterminds
- Control: Medium (dependent on relationship)
- Speed: Medium

**Content marketing (YouTube/LinkedIn/newsletter):**
- Reach: High eventually
- CAC: High initially (content takes time to compound)
- Fit: Medium — wholesale operators aren't heavy LinkedIn users; some are YouTube consumers
- Control: High
- Speed: Slow — 6–12 month horizon

**Referral/affiliate (existing customers bring in others):**
- Reach: Scales with customer base
- CAC: Very Low
- Fit: Very High — operators trust other operators more than any marketing
- Control: High
- Speed: Slow to start, then compounds

**Recommendation for Gunner's current stage:** Lead with **direct outreach** to build the first 20 customers. Simultaneously invest in **mastermind partnerships** for speed-to-audience. Build a **referral program** from day one so early customers have structural incentive to refer. Defer content marketing until there are 25+ customers generating case studies.

### NAH Channel Analysis

NAH's channels are different — seller acquisition, not SaaS acquisition. The principles are the same.

**One channel rule (wholesale playbook):** Pick the channel that's working and squeeze it before adding another. Right now, if BatchDialer + PPL is working, don't add cold SMS yet. Master the working channel completely, then layer.
