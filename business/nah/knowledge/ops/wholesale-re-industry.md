# Wholesale Real Estate — Industry Call Intelligence

This document defines universal truths about motivated seller calls in wholesale real estate.
It is injected into every AI analysis as foundational domain knowledge.

---

## What Wholesale Real Estate Is

Wholesalers find distressed or motivated sellers who want to sell quickly, often below market value,
in exchange for speed, certainty, and no hassle. The buyer is typically a cash investor who closes fast.
The seller is NOT selling to a retail buyer — there are no agents, no showings, no MLS.

The value proposition to the seller: **speed, certainty, no repairs, no fees, no hassle.**

---

## What a Motivated Seller Looks Like

Motivated sellers are not always in distress. Motivation comes in many forms:

**High motivation signals:**
- Inherited property (probate, estate sale) — needs to be settled, family pressure
- Divorce or separation — splitting assets, time pressure
- Financial hardship — behind on mortgage, facing foreclosure, can't afford repairs
- Relocation — job change, moving for family, already moved, tired of managing from afar
- Landlord burnout — problem tenant, tired of being a landlord, done with property management
- Health issues — can't maintain property, downsizing, moving to care facility
- Property in disrepair — can't afford to fix it, embarrassed by it, knows it's a problem
- Behind on taxes — facing tax sale, liens accumulating

**Medium motivation signals:**
- Downsizing — kids moved out, too much house, want something smaller
- Upgrading — want to buy something else, need equity out first
- Ready to sell "when the time is right" — no urgency yet but the decision is made
- Investment property not performing — tired of dealing with it

**Low motivation / tire-kicker signals:**
- "Just curious what it's worth"
- Comparing offers to keep leverage
- No real reason to sell — fishing
- Still emotionally attached to the property
- Wants retail price but with wholesale speed (unrealistic expectations)

## How to Read a Call Transcript

### Disposition Rules

**Appointment Set:**
- Seller agreed to a specific date/time for someone to walk through the property
- OR explicitly said "yes come look at it" or "when can you come by"
- Keywords: "Tuesday", "come by", "walk through", "appointment", "meet you there"
- Do NOT classify as appointment set if they just said they'd "think about it"

**Real Conversation (No Appointment):**
- Meaningful exchange happened — seller shared details about situation, property, or timeline
- Call was substantive (not just "not interested, bye") but no apt was set
- Even a short but real conversation counts

**No Answer / Voicemail:**
- Call went to voicemail OR connected but seller didn't engage
- Very short calls (under 30 seconds) with no real exchange
- "I'll call you back" without any real conversation = voicemail-equivalent

**Not Right Now:**
- Seller IS potentially motivated but has a specific reason they can't act right now
- Key distinction: they have a REASON (tenant, family, timing) vs. they're just not interested
- "My tenant is there until April" = Not Right Now (1-month bucket)
- "I'm not ready yet" with no timeline = Not Right Now (1-year bucket)
- "Maybe in a few months" = Not Right Now (4-month bucket)

**Already Sold:**
- Property is under contract OR already sold/closed
- Under contract ≠ sold — if UC, seller may still be reachable if deal falls through
- Ask: "Is it fully closed or just under contract?" — critical distinction

**Wrong Number:**
- Person reached has no connection to the property
- Seller passed away and family has no interest in dealing with it
- Property was sold years ago and contact info is stale

### DEAD vs. NOT RIGHT NOW — Critical Distinction

**DEAD means ONLY:**
1. Confirmed sold and fully closed (not UC)
2. Active legal threat ("I'm calling my lawyer")
3. Property structurally condemned or has zero value (not just ugly)

**NOT DEAD — even if:**
- Seller said "I'm not interested" (circumstances change)
- Seller is low motivation right now
- Seller was rude or dismissive (they may come around)
- Timeline is far out (1-2 years)
- Price gap is large (deals get done when sellers get desperate)

Low motivation is NEVER dead. "Not interested" today does not mean not interested in 6 months.

## Common Seller Objections and What They Mean

| What they say | What it usually means | Right response |
|--------------|----------------------|----------------|
| "I need to think about it" | Needs permission from spouse/family, or isn't convinced yet | Ask what specifically is holding them back |
| "Your price is too low" | Doesn't understand the value prop yet, or has unrealistic expectations | Educate on cash offer value vs. retail hassle |
| "I'm going to list it" | Hasn't decided yet, testing the waters | Ask about timeline, repairs needed |
| "I already have offers" | May be true, may be leverage play | Ask who from, what range, when deciding |
| "Call me in 6 months" | Not urgent but open to the idea | 4-month follow-up, not dead |
| "My tenant is there" | Active obstacle — specific and solvable | 1-month follow-up after tenant leaves |
| "I'm talking to my family" | Family decision — not a solo call | 4-month bucket, come back when decided |
| "I need [X amount]" | Price anchor — may be negotiable | Understand why — mortgage balance, repairs, etc. |

## Timeline Classification Guide

**1-Month signals** (blocking reason, short runway):
- Tenant leaving in X weeks or months
- Waiting on probate to clear
- "Need to move some stuff out first"
- "Give me a month or so"
- "Once we get through the holidays"
- "My brother needs to sign off too"

**4-Month signals** (medium runway, no specific blocker):
- "Maybe in the spring"
- "Probably around summer"
- "In a few months"
- "By the end of the year" (when that's 3-6 months away)
- "I want to see what happens with the market"

**1-Year signals** (long runway or no timeline given):
- "Eventually"
- "Not anytime soon"
- "Maybe next year"
- No timeline mentioned at all — seller gave no window
- "I'm just gathering information"

## What Good LM Calls Look Like (Coaching Reference)

A great qualification call:
1. **Builds rapport fast** — acknowledges the situation, doesn't dive straight into questions
2. **Discovers the real reason** — what's making them consider selling? Pain point?
3. **Gets the timeline** — when do they need/want to be out?
4. **Asks about the property** — condition, repairs needed, mortgage balance
5. **Sets clear next step** — appointment or specific follow-up date
6. **Handles objections** — doesn't fold, reframes the value

Red flags in a call (coaching flags):
- LM talks more than seller
- No open-ended questions
- Doesn't ask why they're selling
- Accepts "I'll think about it" without digging
- Doesn't confirm next step before hanging up
- Doesn't ask "UC or fully sold?" when seller says already sold
